# The new database

The engine will move to a new, cleanly connected database in one planned step, after it serves every screen from today's database. This note designs the core. `schema/next.sql` defines the new schema. Tests load it into its own namespace in a throwaway database with made-up data. The production rebuild follows the [switch plan](system.md#the-switch-plan).

## Records

- **`party`**: every person and company, once, each with its own id. `kind` is `person` or `company`. A person's `npi` is their individual NPI, and a company's its organization NPI. `how_found` says how a lead or client was found.
- **`merged_party`**: what a merged-away duplicate leaves behind: its old id and the record it became. Nothing lists or shows it.
- **`app_user`**: each person who uses the system, today the two partners, one row each with their person's id and the `slug` they sign in under. `user` is a reserved word in PostgreSQL. A user still shows on timelines and deals as their person. Each user's facts will be columns here, or a child table keyed by the user, as they come.
- **`user_preference`**: a user's own settings and notification choices: their time zone, quiet hours and whether notifications reach their devices.
- **`home_mark`**: a user's own mark on an item on their Home: snoozed until a time, dropped with a reason, and whom it waits on and why.
- **`home_update`**: an update about a deal for one user (`update` or `tour_feedback`), with the words shown on Home, the checklist reference or property when applicable, and when it was read.
- **`job`**: the short list of automatic jobs that change rows, such as the mail capture and the publisher, by slug and label.
- **`do_not_contact`**: a person or company nobody may reach out to, in any role, with the reason and an optional until-date.
- **`specialty`**: the list of a doctor's specialties, such as general dentistry. Like roles, a label can be renamed.
- **`outside_id`**: another system's id for a person, company or deal, such as a Salesforce or roster id, or of a row of today's mail for the email it became, kept as import history.
- **`role`**: the short list of roles, such as client, lead, vendor, lender, listing agent and property owner. A user can rename a label without touching any record. A vendor's category, such as lender, is a role whose `category_of` is vendor.
- **`client_vertical`**: the client's recorded practice type and optional subtype, one row per client role.
- **`client_research`**: a client research observation with its kind, result, source, observation date and any expiry. `client_research_fact` keeps each named fact on the observation.
- **`stage`**: each role's list of stages, such as a vendor's or a lead's. Like roles, a label can be renamed.
- **`how_found`**: the short list of how a lead or client was found, such as referral or public records. Like roles, a label can be renamed.
- **`relationship_level`**: the four levels of how well the team knows a vendor, from 0, Prospective, to 3, Core.
- **`introduction`**: one introduction between a vendor and the team, made either way, and the deal it led to.
- **`property`**: a building, by address, with its county and its point on the map once geocoded, and its name, class, type, year built and stories when known.
- **`market`**: a named market, such as a metro area, by the counties it covers (`market_county`).
- **`territory`**: the area someone serves, as a set of `territory_piece` rows.
- **`deal`**: one piece of brokerage work, with its kind, its reason, its deal owner, its market, its closed value, why it is parked, if it is, with who parked it and when, and whether it needs attention.
- **`deal_client_website`**: a deal's client website registration, with the client it was set up for, its unique website name, its password verifier and when it was disabled, if applicable. A deal can have one active registration.
- **`deal_client_website_attempt`**: the active failed-password window for one website.
- **`deal_client_website_grant`**: a short-lived link grant, with the signed-in user who requested it and the website it opens.
- **`deal_kind`**: the list of kinds of transaction, such as lease and purchase. Like roles, a label can be renamed.
- **`deal_reason`**: the list of reasons a client takes space, such as a start-up or a relocation. Like roles, a label can be renamed.
- **`phase`**: the list of deal phases, from negotiating to closed, plus dead. Like roles, a label can be renamed.
- **`deal_phase_change`**: one row each time a deal changes phase, with when, the new phase and the reason. A deal's phase is its latest row.
- **`offer`**: one round of an LOI or other offer on a deal.
- **`claim_type`**: the short list of what a side can claim about its own position, such as best and final. Like roles, a label can be renamed.
- **`offer_claim`**: one claim the side that sent a round made in it, such as "another tenant is looking".
- **`lease`**: a party's lease at a property, optionally naming the deal that negotiated it.
- **`lease_version`**: one sourced set of signed terms, with consecutive versions for amendments.
- **`lease_option`**: one option in a lease version, with its notice deadline and terms.
- **`critical_date`**: a proposed deadline, with its user confirmation and resolution.
- **`document`**: a file, as its name, its key in object storage (Cloudflare R2) and its type, read from the file's own bytes when the engine stores it. The file itself is never in the database.
- **`party_document`**: a file that belongs to a person or company, such as a client's before any deal.
- **`email_document`**: one link for each email a file came with.
- **`activity`**: something that happened: an email, a call, a meeting or a note, with when, who it came from and a summary.
- **`email`**: the email behind an email activity.
- **`email_summary`**: a recorded summary result for that email, with its source, generator and recorded time. `email_current_summary` gives the one current text when any result has text.
- **`next_step`**: what is due next, with its owner, due date and the time it was done.
- **`weekly_review`**: one deal or prospect marked reviewed in one week's Monday review.
- **`commission`**: a deal's commission, once: what an open deal is expected to bring, the gross the brokerage collects, and the closing user's part on this deal when it overrides their ladder.
- **`commission_payment`**: one part of the gross, with when it was invoiced, falls due and was received.
- **`commission_share`**: what goes to someone other than the users, such as a referring agent or the brokerage, as a share of the whole.
- **`commission_tier`**: one tier of a user's ladder: the count of closed deals it starts from, the closing user's part and their partner.
- **`change`**: one row for each insert, update or delete of a row above, with who made it and when.

Each record and link carries `created_at`, when its row was stored, so moving today's records across can keep their dates. Lists such as roles and phases do not, and neither do rows that carry their own time, including activities and their emails, rounds, phase changes, client research and its facts, posts and readings. An outside id has none either. Only a merge changes it, so its stamp's time is when it was imported or last merged.

## Users, jobs and who changed what

Every row a person or a job changes says who last added or changed it, and `changed_at`, when. A person's change sets `changed_by_user_id`. A job's change sets `changed_by_job` instead, and `changed_for_user_id` when it worked for a user, so a person's change is always told from a job's (decision 35). A row names a user or a job, never both and never neither. These rows are the lists of roles, stages, phases, deal kinds, deal reasons, specialties, how found and claim types, parties, their merged-into pointers, and their emails, phone numbers, roles, companies, specialties and do-not-contact rows, outside ids, properties, markets and their counties, territories, their pieces and who serves them, introductions, knows and can-introduce links, deals and their phase changes, properties and parties, rounds and their documents and claims, leases and their options, documents, activity and what it is about, emails, next steps, users' preferences and Home marks, social accounts, ideas, design templates, posts and their text, media and reviews, readings, and commissions, their payments and shares, and ladder tiers.

The engine names who acts once per transaction, never for the whole connection, because a pooled connection serves the next user. A user's save sets `set_config('engine.user_id', <user id>, true)`. A job's save sets `set_config('engine.job', <slug>, true)`, and `engine.user_id` too when it works for a user. A trigger writes the stamp on every insert and update, whatever the statement says, and refuses the change when it names neither. A statement that changes nothing keeps the row's stamp, so a final post stays as it was. A name that is not a user or a job is refused by its key. A removal names who made it too, in change history.

`runNextSave` in `src/next-save.ts` takes who acts as an argument, a partner by `app_user.slug` or a job and the partner it works for, and sets these settings before the save runs. It keeps each save's answer in `save_receipt` under its idempotency key, so a retry, even one running at the same time, gets the first answer back with `replayed: true` and saves nothing again. The same key with different arguments or a different author is refused as `key_reuse`. A deal save names the deal's latest change it saw, from change history, and `changeDeal` refuses it as `version_conflict` when a later change touched one of its fields; a later change to other fields stands, as today's closing save rebases. A refusal carries `current_version`, the deal's latest change id, where today's carries the deal's version. A save that lands over an unseen change to another field answers `rebased: true` with the intervening change ids and no change id, so the app re-reads the deal before its next save; only an answer with nothing in between carries `change_id`. The deal's row lock queues two crossed saves, so the second sees the first. Today's `deal_conflict` table, four rows with no copy, is not brought across: a crossed save is refused, never stored for later. At the switch, the last 30 days of `tool_call` idempotency keys are copied into `save_receipt` with a request hash nothing matches, so a save that landed in the old tables and is retried after the switch is refused as `key_reuse` instead of saving twice, as decision 12 refuses a retry that crosses a save's switch.

The jobs today:

| Job               | Writes                                                                         | Works for                      |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| `intake`          | posts, their text and media, and their files' documents, from the Friday batch | no one                         |
| `mail_capture`    | emails, their activity and what it is about, and attachments                   | the user whose mailbox it read |
| `publisher`       | a post's claim and its published or failed status                              | no one                         |
| `readings`        | readings                                                                       | no one                         |
| `review_close`    | no response, when review closes                                                | no one                         |
| `agent`           | whatever an agent session saves                                                | the user it works for          |
| `lead_stage`      | evidence-backed lead stage proposals and moves                                 | no one                         |
| `candidate_sweep` | unreviewed candidate source refreshes and suppression                          | no one                         |
| `old_system`      | rows the old system's own migrations and exporters last changed                | no one                         |

A user who settles a post stuck in `publishing` is named on the post. In marketing, a text edit, an edit's review and a cancel name a user, as the one who made it or the one a job worked for, and no response is a job's for no one. A sign-in names its user.

Only the owner adds a user or a job, because every change is signed with one. A user who has changed anything stays, so who changed what survives; a teammate who leaves will be a column on the user. Adding a teammate is a person row and a user row. The first user's own person row names them as its author, so that key is checked when the transaction ends. Being a user is not a role on a deal. A deal names its one deal owner, a user, on the deal itself (see "Deals").

Today's actors map across like this. The old `actor` table holds the two partners as `human`, the scheduled jobs and agent sessions as `automation`, and migrations and exporters as `system`. Its `created_by` and `updated_by` columns name an actor and are often empty, and the old `event` rows add a cause and, for an agent, the partner it worked for. Each `human` actor becomes a person with a user row. The owner loads them with the `stamp_change` triggers turned off, so a row keeps its old author and time: its author from `updated_by`, or `created_by` when never updated, and `changed_at` from `updated_at`, or `created_at`. The check that a row names a user or a job still holds during that load. A row a `human` actor last changed names that user. A row an `automation` actor last changed names the job that now does its work, such as `mail_capture` for the old mail filing, or `old_system` when none does, and an agent session's names `agent`; each takes the partner its event names as the user it worked for. A row a `system` actor last changed names `old_system`. A row with no author names `old_system` too. The old columns that name a partner by slug, such as `deal.owner`, name that partner's user.

Every copy loads `schema/copy-attribution.sql` after `next.sql`, with the new schema on `search_path`. Its `copy_attribution(old_type, old_id, author_id, old_field)` returns `changed_by_user_id`, `changed_by_job` and `changed_for_user_id`. Pass `coalesce(updated_by, created_by)` as the author. Omit `old_field` for the whole row, or pass the field whose separate setter is being copied, such as `contact_state`. The latest event is ordered by `mutation_order`, not the date a caller supplied, and contributes only when its actor matches the author. A copy of one known event, such as phase history, calls `copy_event_attribution(author_id, event_id)` instead, which `copy_attribution` uses for its latest event. `ingest_email` maps to `mail_capture`; the registered interactive runtime actors map to `agent`. Other automatic work stays `old_system` until a replacement is known. Only `agent` and `mail_capture` take the event's human sponsor. Missing sponsors stay unknown, and a human or system actor keeps its own mapping regardless of the event's cause. The call and meeting copies share `copy_capture_summary(capture_session_id, is_logged)`, which returns a capture's recoverable summary and its count of text-bearing meeting records. The party and deal copies share `copy_outside_id(source_system, external_key)`, which turns an old import source and key into an outside id's source and value. The activity and deal next-step copies share `copy_next_step_event()`, which pairs next-step notes with their own events using the mutual closest-match rule described under the activity copy. The lead and deal next-step copies share `copy_action_end()`, which returns each ended next action's or post-call action's end state, evidence and refusal, as described under the deal next-step copy. The activity and email copies share `copy_activity_event()`, which names the event that logged each activity, and `copy_activity_link()`, which returns each old activity link with the deal or party it was copied to. Their SQL bodies depend on old tables. Drop `copy_attribution` and then `copy_event_attribution` before dropping the old `public.actor` and `public.event` tables, `copy_capture_summary` before `public.capture_candidate` and `public.phone_call_session`, `copy_next_step_event` before `public.deal_note` and `public.event`, `copy_action_end` before `public.event`, `public.next_action` and `public.capture_post_call_action`, `copy_activity_event` before `public.event`, and `copy_activity_link` before `public.activity`, `public.client`, `public.lead`, `public.vendor` and `public.deal_note`.

## Links

Each link is one row. No record holds a list or a column per role.

- **`party_role`**: what a person or company is to us. One row per role. A vendor role holds its stage, relationship level and relationship partner. A lead role holds its stage and owner.
- **`party_email`** and **`party_phone`**: a person's or company's emails and phone numbers, one row each, with a label and whether it is the primary one.
- **`party_specialty`**: a person's specialties, one row each.
- **`party_company`**: a person at a company, with their title there and the dates they started and left. A person who moves keeps the old row, with its end date, and gets a new one.
- **`party_link`**: who knows whom, or who can introduce the team to whom, between any two people or companies, with the dates it started and ended.
- **`party_territory`**: who serves a territory, with the dates they started and stopped. A user serves as their person.
- **`deal_party`**: who is on a deal, in which role. The client, the listing agent and each vendor are rows here.
- **`imported_deal_party`** and **`imported_deal_owner`**: who was on a deal and left, and who owned it before, as today's system recorded them, each with when it started and ended.
- **`imported_deal_invoiced`**: the date today's system says a deal was invoiced, which often has no commission row to carry it.
- **`deal_property`**: the properties a deal is considering.
- **`offer_document`**: the documents of each round.
- **`activity_about`**: each deal, person or company, or property an activity concerns.

## Rules the database enforces

Each rule has a test in `src/next-database.test.ts` that fails without it. The tests work as the engine's role (see "Who may change what"), so each refusal is a constraint, a trigger or the merge checking values, or a privilege the role lacks.

1. A link to a record that does not exist is refused, and so is deleting a record something still links to.
2. Every role on a person or a deal comes from the role list.
3. The same link is stored once.
4. A person or company is one of the two kinds.
5. A round's number is used once per deal, and a round answers an earlier round of the same deal.
6. A round comes from someone on the deal, in their role on that deal, about a property the deal is considering.
7. A round is never rewritten or removed, not even by emptying the table. Only its status changes, and a merge points it at the survivor. New terms are the next round, and `withdrawn` takes a round back.
8. At most one round per deal is accepted at a time.
9. Rent, NNN, term, TI, free rent, the escalator and days after delivery are never negative.
10. A round's status moves out of `unknown` to any real status, out of `open` to any real status except `superseded`, or from `accepted` to `superseded`. Every other status is final. A round is superseded only when a later round of the same deal is accepted, checked when the change is saved.
11. Rent starts on a date or a number of days after delivery, never both.
12. A person-at-company link joins a person to a company, ends no earlier than it starts, and is stored once.
13. A deal's phase changes are never rewritten or removed, each phase comes from the list, and a move to dead says why.
14. An activity is an email, a call, a meeting or a note. Each `activity_about` row names exactly one deal, party or property, and is stored once.
15. An email's Message-ID is stored once, is never blank, and never has spaces around it. Only an email the owner's copy of today's mail marks imported may lack it, and then its sender, sent time and subject are stored once. An email belongs to an email activity, and each email and file are linked only once. An email is about at least one record, checked when the change is saved, so an email about nothing is refused and so is removing, moving or emptying away its last link.
16. A next step is about exactly one deal, party or property.
17. A social account is on a known platform, its handle is never empty or only whitespace, and a handle is used once per platform, in any case. Its platform account id is never blank, is used once per platform, and never changes once someone has signed in as it.
18. A post is on its account's platform, in a format that platform offers, and is scheduled, publishing, published, failed or cancelled. Only a published post has the platform's post id and its publish time, and that id is stored once per account. A failed or cancelled post says why, and no other post has a reason.
19. A post is never removed, and a published, failed or cancelled post never changes. A retry is a new post.
20. A reading is of a published post, taken no earlier than it was published, of a metric its platform reports, and never negative. It names its source. A reading that could not be taken has a reason and no value, never a zero. A source or reason is never empty or only whitespace.
21. A reading is stored once and never rewritten or removed.
22. Each platform's reported metrics come from the known platforms and metrics, each once, with the time from which posts must have it, and only a schema change adds one.
23. A post starts scheduled, published or failed, moves from scheduled to publishing or cancelled, and from publishing to published or failed. Nothing else moves.
24. A post is scheduled, or claimed for publishing, only when its account's `engine_publishes_from` is at or before its time. None means never. The account's time cannot change while a claim is being saved, and only the owner sets it.
25. A sign-in is as its account's own platform account id, ends after it starts, and is never rewritten or removed.
26. Each platform's formats come from the known platforms and formats, each once, and only a schema change adds one.
27. A post's text versions run 1, 2, 3 with no gap. A later version is an edit by a user, written while the post is scheduled and before its review closes, an hour before its time by the database's clock. Text is never blank, rewritten or removed.
28. A post's media are documents in order, from 1 to 20. A text post has none, and only a photo set has more than one. Each medium is in its post's format, and its file is of the document's own type: an MP4 for a video, a PDF for a document, and a JPEG or PNG otherwise. A medium is never changed or removed.
29. A review is an edit, a cancel or no response, judged by the database's clock. An edit names a text version after the first, and a cancel is of a cancelled post; both name a user, as the one who made it or the one a job worked for, and come before review closes. No response is a job's for no one, comes at or after the close, only for a scheduled post with no other review. A review holds its post until it is saved, so an edit and the job writing no response take turns. Each version is reviewed once, a post is cancelled and gets no response at most once, and a review is never rewritten or removed.
30. A record or link keeps when it was stored.
31. The engine empties no table, removes no history, and changes only the columns that may change.
32. An email address is one address in lower case, with no name around it, and so is an email's sender. A person or company has each address once, labelled work or personal, and at most one primary.
33. A phone number is a plus and digits only (E.164), and a North American one is +1 and ten digits. An extension is digits only. A person or company has each number and extension once, labelled work, mobile or home, and at most one primary.
34. A change a person makes names the user who made it, and records them and the time itself. A user is a person, and only the owner adds one. A text edit, an edit's review, a cancel and a sign-in name a user.
35. A change a job makes names the job, and the user it worked for, if any. A row names a user or a job, never both and never neither, even when the owner loads it with the stamp turned off. A job comes from the list, and only the owner adds one. A statement that changes nothing keeps who last changed the row.
36. An NPI is ten digits ending in its check digit, and one person or company holds it.
37. A specialty is a person's, comes from the specialty list, and is stored once per person.
38. A person or company is marked do not contact once, with a reason that is never blank and never changes. Its optional until-date may change; outreach reads active_do_not_contact, which includes a stop through that date and an indefinite stop forever.
39. An outside id is for exactly one person, company, deal or email, is stored once per source, and the engine never rewrites or removes one. A merge moves it to the survivor. Its source is a lower-case name, and its value has no space around it.
40. A territory piece is exactly one of a whole state, a named market, or a distance of more than zero miles from a base point, and a territory holds each piece once.
41. A state is a two-digit and a county a five-digit Census FIPS code. A latitude is between -90 and 90 and a longitude between -180 and 180, and a property has both or neither.
42. A market's name is never blank and is used once, and a market holds each county once. A territory's name is never blank.
43. Someone serves a territory that exists, ends no earlier than they started, and is linked once per start date.
44. A merge joins two different people or two different companies, neither merged away. It never merges a user away, and never two records that each hold an NPI.
45. A merge moves everything that points at the duplicate, in every table, to the survivor, and removes the duplicate. Where both hold the same link, the survivor's row stays, and so do its primary email and primary phone. Do not contact keeps the longest restriction and its reason. A second merge onto the same records waits for the first.
46. Only a merge leaves a merged-into pointer, and it names a live record, so merges never chain or loop. Nothing new points at a merged-away record, and its id is never used again. Only the engine and the owner run a merge.
47. A vendor's category is a role whose `category_of` is vendor, and it stays one. Only a vendor holds a vendor category, checked when the change is saved.
48. A stage comes from its own role's stage list, and is stored once per role.
49. A relationship level is a vendor role's, one of the four levels, and only a schema change adds or renames a level.
50. An introduction is with a vendor, made by the vendor or the team, of a person or company other than the vendor, and about a deal that exists. One the team made names the user who made it, and one the vendor made names none. Its note is never blank, and who it joins and who made it never change. A vendor keeps its vendor role while it has introductions.
51. A disposition and a target are a vendor role's. A disposition is parked or avoid, and none is active. A parked or avoided vendor has a reason that is never blank, and an active one has none. The owner-run copy can keep a ruling with the reserved reason `not recorded, set before the new system`. New rulings require a recorded reason. A merge keeps the stricter disposition, avoid over parked over none, with its reason. An avoided vendor cannot be a target, and a merge clears its target.
52. Every insert, update and delete of a stamped row leaves one change row: the record's key, the fields it changed from what to what, and who made it and when. A statement that changes nothing leaves none.
53. The engine never adds, rewrites or removes a change, and a removal names who made it. A change is an insert, an update, a delete or an owner-requested erase.
54. A change a merge makes names the merge, and only a merge saved in the same transaction is named.
55. A deal's kind comes from the kind list and its reason from the reason list. Either may be unknown.
56. A deal has exactly one deal owner, a user.
57. A deal's market comes from the market list, and its closed value is more than zero.
58. Lease versions are consecutive on a lease, have a source document, an exact expiration and ordered known dates, and never change or disappear. A supplied offer was accepted when recorded and belongs to the same deal and property. Typed amounts are nonnegative, term is positive and rent starts on a date or after delivery, never both.
59. Every lease option is numbered once on its version, is renewal, expansion or early termination, and has an exact notice deadline and nonblank terms. An option notice is no later than the lease ends. Options never change or disappear; sourced options may be added to any version as they are extracted.
60. A critical date starts proposed, with a known kind and nonblank source; its source round belongs to its deal, and its source lease version or option is on a lease the deal negotiated or renews. Repeated generated proposals are stored once.
61. Only a direct user confirms or resolves a critical date. The database records who and when, retains confirmation through resolution, and refuses all other transitions, rewrites and removals. Resolution needs a nonblank reason.
62. A lead has a stage. Lead and vendor roles may name an owner, who must be a user.
63. A lead or client says how they were found, from the list. A new lead or client is checked when the change is saved. Only a record copied from the old system may say it was not recorded, checked when the change is saved.
64. Someone on a deal as its client holds the client role, checked when the change is saved, so the role cannot be removed while they are.
65. A client profile names its client role, has only roster, cold, engaged or paused as manual status, and takes its type from the client type list. Active deal and past client come from deals.
66. A brand membership links a client to a different party that is a company. A client's membership dates never overlap.
67. A national account is a company holding that role. Its dated owners are Joe or Dell, and their dates never overlap on one account. Membership and ownership end strictly after they start, with the end excluded.
68. Client and account profiles and their dated links keep their parties; users may correct dates and remove mistakes. A merge carries a lone profile and refuses two client profiles or two account profiles rather than discarding their histories.
69. A parked deal says why: the client paused, the prospect was never active, or another reason. Only a parked deal has a note, and a note is never blank. Only a parked deal has a time it was parked, and a user who parked it only with that time.
70. Needs attention is never unknown. Only a user, or a job working for one, sets or clears it on a deal or lead, or parks or revives a deal. Other party roles cannot have the flag.
71. Lead stage evidence names a lead and two different lead stages. Non-archive moves name an activity about that lead and nonblank evidence. Repeated proposals and applied evidence for the same activity and move are refused. Pending moves use the shared proposal queue. An unattended job applies only strong evidence along the funnel; weak evidence and every archive wait for a direct user, and archiving has a nonblank reason. Applying a stale proposal is refused. Applied evidence is final and never rewritten or removed.

72. A lead's lane comes from its list. A lease estimate has both date and nonblank source, and confidence only with an estimate. Lane, estimate, confidence and segment belong only to the lead role. How-found detail is nonblank and has how found.
73. A lease names its tenant and property, with an optional originating deal. A negotiated lease is for that deal's client and property, and only one lease originates from a deal. An outside lease needs no invented deal. Lease identities never change or disappear.
74. A renewal reason and the lease it renews are required together. The source lease exists, and its originating deal is not the renewal deal. A user may correct the reason and source together.

## Who may change what

`schema/next.sql` defines `engine`, the role the engine will use on this database. A login takes it with `grant engine to <login>`. The engine connects with `role=engine` set, or as a login created in SQL. A role made in the Neon console belongs to `neon_superuser`, and its own rights would bypass these grants. Loading the schema refuses an existing `engine` role that is a member of any role, is a superuser, bypasses row security, or can create roles or databases or replicate.

The role reads rows in every table, adds rows in every table except `app_user`, `job`, `merged_party`, `change`, `imported_change`, `email_summary`, `platform_metric`, `platform_format` and `relationship_level`, and empties none. A social account it adds has no `engine_publishes_from`, so removing an account and adding it back cannot set one. A text version and a review it adds take the stamp's time, the clock's, which no statement sets. It removes rows only outside history, and changes only these columns:

- a role's, a stage's, a phase's, a deal kind's, a deal reason's, a specialty's, a how-found's or a claim type's label;
- a vendor role's stage, relationship level, relationship partner, disposition, reason and target, a lead role's stage, owner and Needs attention flag, an introduction's date, deal and note, and a knows or can-introduce link's dates and note;
- a party's, market's, territory's, deal's, document's or design template's name, a party's NPI and how found, and a property's address, county and point;
- a deal's kind, reason, lease it renews, deal owner, market, closed value, parking reason and note, and whether it needs attention;
- a person-at-company link's title and dates, and a territory link's dates;
- an email's or phone number's label and whether it is primary;
- an activity's time, sender and summary;
- a next step's text, owner, due date and done time;
- a user's time zone, quiet hours and device notifications, a Home mark's disposition, snooze time, drop reason and whom it waits on and why, and a Home update's `read_at` (`read_at` is the only updatable column on `home_update`);
- a round's status, and a critical date's status and resolution reason;
- a do-not-contact row's until-date;
- a social account's handle and platform account id, and an idea's angle, hook and pillar;
- a post's status, reason, platform post id and publish time, until it is published, failed or cancelled;
- a commission's expected amount, gross and override, a payment's amount and dates, a share's size, and a ladder tier's closing part.

History is `document`, `document_extraction`, `critical_date`, `lease`, `lease_version`, `lease_suite`, `lease_option`, `deal_phase_change`, `offer`, `offer_document`, `offer_claim`, `post` and its `post_text`, `post_media` and `post_review`, `measurement`, `social_sign_in`, `outside_id` and `change`. Ids, `created_at`, and the records a link or an about row joins never change; a different link is a removed row and a new one. Who an activity came from and who owns a next step can be corrected. PostgreSQL refuses anything else before it touches a row, so a save runs no trigger just to be told no. Besides the stamp that writes who changed a row and the history that records it, the triggers left check values: a deal parked, revived or flagged by a user, a round's status moves, a later acceptance superseding a round, a lease's round accepted when recorded, a critical date's user confirmation and resolution, an option notice before lease expiration, an email about a record, a post's status moves, a post that is final, the engine's start time for an account, an edit or review inside its window, a reading of a published post, and a merged-away id used again.

A merge is the one path that rewrites history: `merge_party` runs with the owner's rights and points rounds and outside ids at the survivor. Each row it moves is stamped with whoever ran it, so a moved row's stamp no longer names who first stored it. Its change history still does, and names the merge.

The owner's own login can still change anything. That path is not the engine's concern.

## Emails and phone numbers

A person or company can have several emails and phone numbers. Each is a row in `party_email` or `party_phone` with a label, and one of each may be primary. A changed address or number is a removed row and a new one.

Each is stored in one form, so a repeated address or number is caught (decision 32). The database checks the form and does not convert; whoever writes a row converts first. An email address is lower case ASCII, with no display name. A phone number is E.164, such as `+12175550100`, and an extension such as "x12" is its own column. The same address or number can be on two parties, such as a shared front desk. Two parties that turn out to be one person are merged (see "Merging duplicates").

An email's `sender` is stored in the same form, so the agent finds who sent it by matching `sender` to `party_email.address`, and sets the activity's `from_party_id`. The rows are keyed address first, so that match is a lookup.

Today's `party` row holds one free-text `email`, `phone` and `cell` on each person and company, as typed. Moving them across:

- `phone`, the office line, becomes a work number, primary. `cell` becomes a mobile number, primary when there is no `phone`.
- A ten-digit number, with or without a leading 1 and in any punctuation, becomes +1 and its ten digits. A trailing extension moves to `extension`.
- `email` sometimes holds several addresses, or a name with the address in angle brackets. Each address becomes its own work row, lower case and trimmed, and the first is primary.
- The same number or address twice becomes one row. `cell` is primary when no `phone` row is stored.
- A value that fits neither form is not stored. The move lists it for a partner to fix.

## Users' preferences and Home marks

The owner ruled on #196 that each user's settings, Home marks and notification choices become per-user preferences on the user (2026-11-16). Both tables are keyed by the user, so the owner still adds users while the engine keeps their preferences.

- **Preferences.** `user_preference` holds one row per user: their `time_zone`, an IANA name such as `America/Chicago` that the database knows; quiet hours, `quiet_from` and `quiet_until`, in that zone, which may run past midnight; and `push_to_device`, whether notifications reach their devices. The known zones come from the server's time zone data, and PostgreSQL does not recheck stored zones when that data changes, so a server upgrade checks that every stored zone is still known. A user with no row has chosen nothing, and a read uses the defaults.
- **Home marks.** A mark snoozes an item until `snoozed_until`, drops a call follow-up or Home update for good with a nonblank `drop_reason`, or says whom an item waits on, a person or company, with the reason. Waiting is separate from snoozing, as today, and is cleared on drop. A dropped mark cannot be changed or deleted. Exactly one of `next_step_id`, `call_follow_up_id`, `home_update_id`, `proposal_id` or `reply_draft_id` is set. The database gives each step just one of the two mark keys and gives each user one mark per item. An update's mark belongs to its recipient and a draft's mark belongs to its owner.
- **Call follow-ups.** A follow-up is the next step itself. It links to its recorded call on the same deal and keeps the evidence quote. Home reads the call's date and summary from `activity` and the contact from the saved follow-up or the call when known. The same step owns the due date, completion and reopen.
- **Updates.** `home_update` holds the recipient, deal, `kind` (`update` or `tour_feedback`), wording and creation time. Checklist reminders (`update`) require the checklist item reference and due date and are unique per recipient, deal, item and due date. Tour feedback (`tour_feedback`) forbids checklist references and may name a `property_id`. An update with `read_at` has already been acknowledged. The checklist reference or property lets Home open the deal at that item.

Moving today's rows across:

- Each `ops.notification_preference` row becomes its actor's user's preferences: `timezone`, `quiet_hours_start` and `quiet_hours_end`, and `device_opt_in` as `push_to_device`. A zone the database does not know, or equal start and end, is listed for that user to fix. `version` does not move; change history replaces it.
- Each `ops.home_inbox_disposition` that is snoozed on a `next_action:` or `capture_post_call_action:` item becomes a mark on the next step it became. Dispositions match by `item_id` or `v2_item_id`, preferring an exact `item_id`, then the latest change and then `item_id`, as the old Home does. An `inbox` or `settled` row is no mark and overrides inherited snoozes. Of the 11 old hidden marks, 5 come back as inbox cards, and the other 6 have no live card (one cancelled step and five notifications with no copied card) (owner, 2026-11-19; `docs/screens/home-inbox.md`). A post-call action uses the call follow-up key. Marks on call sessions, reply drafts and update groups wait for slice 10. `source_version`, `material_version` and `explicit_unsettle` do not move.
- Each `ops.home_waiting` with someone named becomes the mark's `waiting_on_party_id`, an actor as their person, and its `reason`. Its `evidence` lines do not move. A row whose identity was cleared waits on no one.
- `ops.home_inbox_batch` and the `_write` tables are receipts of past saves and do not move.
- Feature switches (`feature_switch`) are switches for releasing old-system features, and `system_config` holds system-wide values. Neither is a user's setting, so neither moves here; the owner decides them separately.

## Do not contact, NPIs and outside ids

Do not contact is a fact about the person or company, whatever roles they hold, so a lead, a client and a vendor contact are all covered by one row. The owner: "the only time we would make someone do not contact it would be for any reason." Its row is keyed by the party alone, so any outreach finds it with the party's id. It carries the reason, and its stamp says which user or job set it and when. The reason never changes. Changing its until-date restamps it, and history keeps the earlier stamp; lifting it removes the row.

An NPI is checked for shape: ten digits, the last a Luhn check digit over the prefix 80840 and the first nine. The first digit is not checked, so tests use made-up numbers starting with 9, which no NPI issued yet does. NPPES issues each number once, to a person or an organization, so the number is unique across both. A doctor's specialties are rows in `party_specialty`; a company has none.

Old reference ids, such as Salesforce ids and roster numbers, are kept in `outside_id` to trace where a record came from and open existing links. They are not party or deal columns. A source and value are stored once, so a repeated import finds the record it already made. The lead copy also stores copied lead IDs under `old_system_lead` so the Leads board can identify them separately from other IDs on the same party. Only a merge changes a row, so its stamp names the job or user that imported it, or the one that last merged it, and when.

## Clients, brands and national accounts

A `client` row extends its party's client role with a manual status and a `client_type` from the short list. The initial types are independent, group practice, DSO, franchise and regional system. Labels may change, like roles. Only roster, cold, engaged and paused are stored. `client_status` reports active_deal when any client deal's latest phase is neither closed nor dead, including a deal with no phase yet. Otherwise any closed phase in its history makes it past_client, and otherwise the manual status applies. An open deal wins over past work. A dead deal alone does not make a past client.

A brand is a company. As the owner decided on 2026-11-18, a person who is a client may also belong to a national account (#697). `client_brand` links a person or company with a client profile to a different brand company, with dates. Its dates include started_on and exclude ended_on, and an absent end means still a member. Memberships never overlap, even across brands. Ending one and starting the next on the same day preserves which brand held the location on each date. A franchisee has its own client row and franchise type; its deals name it, never its brand.

A `national_account` is a company with that role. It need not itself be a client. `national_account_owner` names Joe or Dell as an app user, with dates under the same non-overlap rule. Account ownership is independent of deal ownership. The engine can correct membership and ownership dates and remove mistaken links; change history keeps what was corrected. Changing the parties removes the mistaken link and adds the right one. All five tables are stamped and their changes recorded. A merge moves a lone profile and its dated links; two client profiles or two account profiles are refused for a user to settle first, as are overlapping histories or a location becoming its own brand.

## Deal files and extracted text

Deal files extend the shared `document` table. `deal_id` identifies the deal and `kind` describes the file, such as `loi`, `lease`, `amendment`, `invoice` or `other`. `document_kind` is a short list with labels a user may rename, like deal kinds. Several independent files may have the same kind. Party files and post media can use this table without a deal or kind.

A stored deal file has its bucket and R2 key, content type, SHA-256 hash and byte count. The bytes remain in R2. `r2_bucket` is a configured bucket alias. `post_media`, the default for existing intake, means the POST_MEDIA binding in the current environment; `deal_documents` means the legacy `carr-documents` bucket. The pair of bucket and key identifies one file, so identical keys in different buckets do not collide. Each replacement is a new document with the next `version` and `supersedes_id` pointing at its predecessor on the same deal and kind. Independent files start at version 1. A unique predecessor prevents two replacements of one version. The engine may rename a document. An existing attachment may be filed to a deal once, supplying its kind and any missing content type, hash and size together. Known metadata must stay unchanged. Apart from the one-time classification below, the database refuses later changes to these fields, removal of its deal, or changes to its lineage or storage location. Existing offer links and lease source documents use these same rows.

A direct user may classify a filed `other` document as a real kind once. That update changes only `kind`; all other stored fields stay fixed, including its display name. The usual name-only rename remains available separately. A real kind never changes through this path, including back to `other`. Jobs cannot classify, even for a user. The existing version links still require the same deal and kind, so classify every version of an `other` chain in one update; classifying only one version is refused. Shared change history records the old and new kind, the user and when they saved it (decision 78, rule 97).

`document_extraction` stores a completed attempt on one deal file version. It keeps the extraction time, method, extractor version, optional model, text format version, status, ordered page strings and optional JSON receipt. Empty page strings preserve page positions. `produced`, `no_text` and `unavailable` distinguish usable text, an empty text layer and an unsuccessful extraction. `receipt_only` retains a fact-extraction receipt without claiming to supply page text. A different extractor, model, method or text format creates another attempt; repeating the same combination is refused, including a null model. The document's immutable hash identifies the bytes extracted. Shared stamps and change history retain who stored each row and when, separately from the extraction time. These rows do not approve extracted business facts.

Moving today's rows across:

- Finalized `ops.deal_deliverable` rows supply filenames, object keys, MIME types, byte counts, hashes and dates. Upload grants, expiry, visibility and unfinished uploads stay behind. Load LOI, counter, lease, amendment, invoice and other before copying. Copy known generated files using the reviewed template-to-kind mapping. Use `other` and list the file for classification when no kind is known.
- Deal `attachment` rows supply the files behind extracted text and fact receipts. The copy lists soft-deleted attachments by ID and skips their files and derivatives. Rows that name one storage key describe one file. After validating those rows, the copy keeps the earliest filing for each SHA-256 hash on a deal, including its ID, name and R2 key. For distinct files with the same name and kind on one deal, the copy assigns versions and links each version to its predecessor in filed order (#828, decision 186).
- Each `ops.deal_document_text` row becomes an extraction attempt with its page strings, status, format version and creation time as extraction time. Its source hash must match the document. The legacy `page-text.v1` pipeline uses method `text_layer` and extractor version `page-text.v1`, with no model. Recompute any text digest from retained pages rather than carrying a second text copy.
- Each `ops.deal_extraction_job` maps its attachment to that same document version. Preserve its id, actor stamp, recorded time, contract's extractor version and model, method `legacy_fact_receipt` to identify the imported receipt rather than infer whether it used OCR, and full contract and receipt inside the receipt object. Jobs contain fact receipts rather than page text, so use text format `fact-receipt.v1`, status `receipt_only` and empty pages. Keep any separate page-text attempt as well. A missing source, hash mismatch or conflicting extraction identity is listed for correction. Fact batches and approvals belong to their own later slice.
- Today's generated `document` points at working and PDF attachments. Each surviving attachment becomes a document; the old generated id is an `outside_id` on its deal, qualified by source `old_generated_document`. Each of its files keeps its note as written, and a blank note stays empty. Template definitions, lint results and delivery workflow are not copied by this slice. Client-only generated records are copied by `copy-client-files.sql` without assigning a deal. Generated records with neither a deal nor a client remain listed as `no_deal`.

Keeping documents also prevents deleting an email with attachments or a deal with filed files; the engine must retain those source records. This applies to attachments and post media as well as deal files.

This slice designs storage only. Upload, extraction jobs, reads and the eventual copy run follow separately. It changes no live data.

## Client files

The old system files a generated document to its client when no deal exists (carr-system `pipelines/backfill_document_attachments.py`, an `attachment` whose subject is the client). `party_document` gives such a file its place: one row joins a person or company to a `document`. The link is the party's, not a role's, so a file stays put when the party gains or loses the client role.

- **One file, stored once.** The bucket and R2 key still identify one `document`. A file several parties share, such as a brand's file its locations use, is one row with a link per party. A party file may also be filed to a deal, once, as any stored attachment is; it keeps its party links and the deal's kind and version rules then apply. The same party and file are linked once.
- **Missing files.** A link needs a stored file: a nonblank type, a hash and a size. A row with only a name and key cannot be linked, so a party never shows a file whose bytes were not stored. A file whose bytes are gone, like an old generated document with neither file on disk, gets no document at all; the copy lists it for a user instead (rule 75).
- **Merging duplicates.** A merge moves the duplicate's links to the survivor. Where both hold the same file, the survivor's link stays and the duplicate's is removed; change history keeps it, told as that merge's.
- **Corrections.** The engine may remove a mistaken link; the file stays, since documents are history. A link never changes; a different link is a removed row and a new one. Links are stamped and their changes recorded.

A party file has no kind. Kinds and versions stay deal-file facts until the owner asks for kinds on client files.

`schema/copy-client-files.sql` runs after the party and deal-file copies in its own owner transaction, after `copy-attribution.sql`. It only reads `public`, moves no bytes, and ends with a correction list. A rerun leaves files, links and history unchanged. A client-only generated file keeps its note, as a deal file does.

Moving today's rows across:

- A live `attachment` whose subject is a client becomes a document in bucket `deal_documents`, keeping its id, name, key, type, hash, size and time, linked to the client's party through the party map, including a merged-away client's survivor. Rows naming one key are one file, as in the deal-file copy.
- A generated `document` with a client and no deal links its surviving working and PDF files the same way. One with neither file, a deleted attachment, a client that did not copy, and rows of one key that disagree on type, hash or size are listed, not linked.

## Offer rounds

Every LOI, counter and response is a new `offer` row. It holds its round number, the round it answers, who sent it and in what role, when, and its status: `open`, `countered`, `accepted`, `rejected`, `withdrawn` or `superseded`. Only an owner's import may set `unknown`, with `import_source`, `import_id` and the original `import_record`. The engine cannot insert or change those provenance columns, even when acting as the old-system job. Unknown can resolve to any real status, including superseded only with a later acceptance. No status returns to unknown. The original record stays unchanged after resolution. The standard terms are typed columns, so the app can show what changed from one round to the next: rent and estimated NNN per square foot per year, term in months, TI per square foot, free rent months, yearly escalation percent, and when rent starts, as a date or as a number of days after delivery. Each later term is one more column. An optional `expires_on` records an LOI's stated expiry; only an open round proposes that deadline.

A round can also carry what its side claimed about its own position, one `offer_claim` per claim type, such as best and final, an authority limit ("the owner will not go below 34") or a competing interest ("another tenant is looking"), as the owner ruled on #196. A side often makes several claims at once, so each is its own row. A claim names no person: the round already says who sent it. `floor_rent_per_sf_yr` holds the rent a side says it will not go past, when it differs from the round's own. `quote` keeps their words and `note` ours. A deadline is the round's `expires_on`, never a claim. Claims are history with their round, as its documents are: a claim heard after the round is saved, such as on a later call, is added then, and none is changed or removed. Whether a later round went past a claim is read from the rounds that follow, never stored.

Moving today's rows across, each `negotiation_claim` becomes an `offer_claim` on the round its `round_id` became, with its type, quote, note and author. Today's claim types become the list, except `deadline`, which today's rows cannot hold either. `falsifiable`, `reversal_test` and `sort` serve today's bluff scorecard, which is not carried, so they do not move. A `stated_floor` moves when its basis is `usd_sf_yr`, and one in `usd_sf_mo` moves times 12. A monthly, yearly or total amount needs the space's size, and a total per square foot needs the term, so those claims are listed for a partner. A `source` other than `stated` is listed too.

Each round can also record how tight its submarket was when it was sent, in `submarket_condition`: `soft`, `balanced` or `tight`, as the coordinator ruled on #313. It sits on the round, not the deal, because a submarket can move during a negotiation, such as when another tenant appears. Null means not recorded, never balanced. Like the round's terms, it is never changed. A deal's current condition is its latest round that records one, read and never stored. Moving today's rows across, each `negotiation_round.submarket_condition` becomes the condition of the round it became, unchanged, and any other value is listed for a partner. Today's list holds the same three slugs; its labels, notes and signed tightness number served the bluff scorecard and do not move.

`everything_about_deal` returns a deal's phase changes in order, its properties, its parties with their roles and recorded cities, its rounds in order with their documents and claims, its activity in time order with each email and its attachments, its next steps by due date, its confirmed open critical dates and its proposals awaiting confirmation, each by due date then id, and who was on it and owned it before, in one read. The property reaches the deal through `deal_property`, and the client and the listing agent through `deal_party`. `earlier_parties` and `earlier_owners` are the copied periods from `imported_deal_party` and `imported_deal_owner` in the order they ended, each earlier person with their `party`. `history` contains the complete `change` rows for the deal record and its `deal_party` rows, so a native owner handoff and a native removal both show there; `imported_history` contains the deal record's complete `imported_change` rows, including the copied owner changes. Both sort by `changed_at` then id. Other child-row changes, such as phases and properties, stay out. `imported_deal_history` is the same read-only list the deal page receives for old events with no other home.

`everything_about_party` returns `party` with its recorded city, a nullable `do_not_contact` restriction with its reason, end date and derived `active` flag, true through its end date, a nullable `outreach_draft` without its fixed send flags, and nineteen lists: `emails`, `phones`, `companies` (dated person/company links), `roles` (stage and lead owner, with the client profile and derived `current_status` on the client role), `specialties`, `territories` (dated, each with its `pieces` and their `market`), `vendor_verticals`, `vendor_loan_programs`, `deals` (one entry per role), `links` (knows and can-introduce in either direction), `introductions` (completed, from either end), `brands` (location/brand memberships), `national_account_owners` (account/person ownership), `leases`, `activity` (explicit party links only), `next_steps`, pending `proposals`, `history` and `imported_history`. Company, relationship, introduction, brand and ownership links keep their endpoint ids and embed the other endpoint as `party`. Leases include the property and all signed `versions`, each with its `document`, `suites` and `options`. Both party and deal reads use `activity_entry` for activity with each email and its attachment `documents`. A merged-away id reads its survivor; a missing id returns SQL null, like the deal read. Every list is `[]` when empty. Emails and phones put primary first, then address or number and extension; company, brand and ownership links sort by start date, roles and specialties by slug, territories by start date, pieces by state, market name and distance, verticals and loan programs by name, deals by name and id then role, relationship links by kind and start date, introductions by date, leases by property address, versions by version number, suites by label, options by number, activity by time, next steps by due date and proposals by creation time. Nullable dates, suite labels and piece fields sort last and ids break ties wherever rows have ids. Dated history, expired restrictions and completed next steps remain visible. `history` contains the complete `change` rows for the party record and every duplicate merged into it; `imported_history` contains their complete `imported_change` rows, preserving each original `source_event`. Both sort by `changed_at` then id. Child-row changes, such as emails and roles, stay out.

## Owner decision: unusual one-off terms

Some LOIs carry a term no other deal has, such as an exclusive-use clause. There are two ways to keep it:

- **A plain text field on each round.** This is built and recommended. It keeps one place, is easy to read, and the document holds the exact wording.
- **A row per extra term**, each with a name and a value. Each term could then be compared round to round, at the cost of another table and a name for every term.

Unusual terms are rare and are read by people, not computed, so the text field is enough.

## When a deal falls apart

A round's status records what happened to that round, and the deal's phase records where the deal stands. An accepted round stays accepted when the deal later dies. The deal moves to the dead phase, with a date and a reason, and nothing is deleted. If talks restart, the deal moves to a live phase again and the new rounds answer the last one. When a new round is accepted, the earlier accepted round becomes `superseded` in the same save, so a deal has one current accepted round. An accepted round is never superseded on its own. A mistaken status is fixed by a new round, as a mistaken term is.

## Activity, emails and next steps

- **`activity`** records something that happened. **`activity_about`** links it to each deal, person or company, or property it concerns, one row each. One email about a deal, its client and its lender shows on all three timelines. `activity.reached` records whether the person was reached: true means reached, false means attempt only, and null means unknown. It is never inferred from the summary.
- **`email`** holds the message's own Message-ID header (Outlook's `internetMessageId`), never an id one mailbox gives it, so mail both users received is stored once. Only today's mail copied without it lacks one. The id is unique, keeps its case, and is refused when blank or space-padded. The row also holds the Outlook conversation id when known, the sender, the subject and the new text only, with quoted history removed. The date is the activity's time. The agent stores only an email it ties to a record. Mail that matches nothing stays in the mailbox and is not copied, and the database refuses an email that is about no record. The original mailbox stays the full record.
- **Attachments** go to R2. Each becomes a `document` that names the email it arrived with.
- **Email summaries.** An `email_summary` belongs to one stored email. The database refuses to delete an email while it has any summary results. Each available result has its original text. An unavailable result records that it had no text. Each result identifies its source activity, captured-content hash and generator version, and keeps the recorded provenance (including the model when recorded) and time. The database view `email_current_summary` gives at most one text per email. It picks the latest available result by recorded time, breaking ties by source activity id, hash and generator version. An email with only unavailable results has no current text (decision 177).
- **`next_step`** is about exactly one deal, person or company, or property. Its owner is a party. A deal checklist item also keeps `item_title`, ordered `item_bullets` and `source_ref`. A titled item requires a deal, a two-to-four-word title, a nonblank source and an array of bullets, checked by the database. Untitled steps default to no title, empty bullets and no source. Its generated `status` is `resolution`, or open when that is null, so each state has one encoding. Done can have no known completion time. Only done has `done_at`; only cancelled or expired has `resolved_at`, nullable when the actual decision time is unknown. Cancelled requires a nonblank `resolution_reason`, and other states have none. `resolution_event_id` optionally holds the UUID of the old event that closed the step. Only the owner's import can insert or update it; open steps cannot have it. New app cancellations are recorded in change history. All ended steps remain in detail reads. A held step's `hold_until` is the first day it may show on Home, in Central time as today's system counts it. `home_next_step` includes only open steps past their hold. The Home inbox also reads held steps for In motion and waiting, and recent steps with a known completion time for Done. Detail reads show held steps with their hold.

The copy retains each source row's id, text, owner and due date alongside its end state. If its owner is missing or was an automation, use the copied deal or client owner, or the vendor's relationship partner. A vendor with no relationship partner follows the creator and last-work rules in "Copying today's deal and vendor next steps". Lead steps without a partner owner follow the lead's copied claim. Without a responsible partner the step stays unclaimed. The database still allows each person one open untitled next step per deal or client, independently of checklist items. A dropped `public.next_action` maps to cancelled using the matching `drop-action` event's `new_value.reason` or `human_quote`, tied by `next_action_id`, with the event id in `resolution_event_id` and its actual `occurred_at` in `resolved_at`. A cancelled step with no recorded reason keeps the reason `not recorded before this system`. Its time and event id stay empty unless one matching drop event proves them. Conflicting recorded reasons or drop evidence still wait for correction. An expired action maps to expired, with the matching `clear-the-deck-apply` event id in `resolution_event_id` when its item proves the expiry. Several steps can name the same deck event. Use its decision time only when the item proves that expiry; a stale deadline is not an expiry decision. Without reliable time or evidence, leave those fields null and list the gap. Done actions keep an explicit completion time when present, such as a post-call action's `completed_at` or the matching `complete-one-action` event's `occurred_at` for a `next_action`; otherwise record done with a null `done_at`. Neither a row's last edit nor the copy time proves completion. The deal, vendor, lead and client copies follow this (#479, #480).

A row that may point at one of several kinds of record has one checked key per kind and a check that exactly one is set. It never holds a type word and an id the database cannot check (decision 21). Activity and next steps are not history: a note can be corrected, and a step is marked done.

Size, as reasoning: a stored email is a few kilobytes of plain text, without attachments or quoted history, and only mail about a record is kept. So the table grows with deal work, not with mailbox traffic. Files grow in R2.

## Worked example: an LOI response by email

The partners toured 100 Example Way with their client and sent an LOI. The listing agent emails back with a counter.

1. The client and the listing agent are each a `party`, with `party_role` rows for client and listing agent.
2. The property is a `property`, linked to the deal by `deal_property`.
3. On the deal, `deal_party` holds the client as client and the agent as listing agent.
4. Our LOI is round 1, sent by the client.
5. The agent's email becomes an `activity` from the agent, with its `email` row and `activity_about` rows for the deal and the agent.
6. The attached PDF goes to R2 and becomes a `document` that names the email.
7. The counter is round 2. It comes from the agent as listing agent, answers round 1, carries its own terms, and links the PDF in `offer_document`. Round 1 becomes countered.
8. Our response is round 3. The agent's acceptance marks it accepted.
9. A `next_step` on the deal says to start the lease draft.

The tests run rounds 1 to 3, the acceptance, the email with its attachment, a call and the next steps, and read them back in order.

## Leases and options

A `lease` identifies its tenant party and property. Its optional `deal_id` names the brokerage work that negotiated it; when present, its tenant is the deal's client and its property is on the deal. A lease negotiated elsewhere has no deal. One negotiated deal produces one lease, and both kinds use the same `lease_version` terms and `lease_option` rows.

A `lease_version` has its source document and exact expiration. Execution and commencement dates remain null when unknown; dates are never inferred from term months. Rent and NNN are per square foot per year, TI per square foot, and escalation a yearly percent. `rent_starts_on` or `rent_days_after_delivery` keeps rent commencement separate from the lease's start. Signed terms are independent of the LOI. An optional offer names a round accepted when recorded, on the lease's negotiating deal and property; outside leases have none.

Amendments and corrections are consecutive versions of the same lease. `current_lease` selects the latest version, including tenant, property and optional originating deal, so every sourced lease ending can be queried by `expiration_on`. `lease_ending` is the work list; it excludes an old lease once a renewal deal has a new signed version. The lease identity and existing versions and options are immutable. Source-backed options may be added later to any version. An option's `lease_version_id` names its exact version, with its kind, notice deadline and terms. Notices cannot follow expiration, but can predate an amendment retaining them.

A renewal is a new deal with `reason = 'renewal'` and `renews_lease_id` pointing at the lease it renews. Both fields are required together, and it cannot renew a lease negotiated by that same deal. Its client may be a successor tenant, such as after a practice acquisition, and need not be the old tenant. Its offers and new lease belong to the new deal; the old lease and its negotiation history stay. Critical date proposals can refer to a version of either the lease the deal negotiated or the one it renews. A renewal proposes the old lease's end and option notices, never its historical rent start.

Moving today's rows across, each deal's lease chain becomes one lease and its ordered versions. A lease without brokerage work names only its tenant and property. An old renewal deal becomes renewal in the live deal record when its source lease is known. The deal copy keeps the historical Renewal label for ended deals with an unrecorded source lease and lists the missing lease in the move report. Preserve exact known dates and source evidence as documents; list missing expiration or evidence for a partner to fix. Known executed dates stay, unknown ones stay null. Convert a legacy rent quote only when its unit is known; otherwise preserve its wording in `other_terms`. Options in `options_note` need individual terms and sourced deadlines before becoming option rows; preserve unparsed wording in `other_terms`. The old radar's current ending becomes `current_lease.expiration_on`, including outside leases; the 24-month radar and 18-month renewal windows remain read filters on `lease_ending`.

## Critical dates

A `critical_date` starts proposed, with a deal, kind, due date and nonblank source. It can name its source round, lease, lease option or document; a round or lease must belong to that deal. Kinds are LOI expiry, option notice, lease commencement (`lease_commencement`), rent start, lease end, due diligence, phase effective date or other. The source text describes manually added dates too. Lease commencement has its own kind under the owner ruling of 2026-11-19.

`propose_critical_dates(deal_id)` derives dates from current lease versions and their options, and expiries on open rounds. Repeating it adds nothing twice. It uses exact dates only; days after delivery need an actual delivery date before a rent-start proposal can be made. New amendments produce new proposals; old proposals and confirmed dates remain for a user to drop when appropriate.

A direct user confirms a proposal to open, or drops it with a reason. Only a direct user resolves an open date as done, dropped or passed, with a reason. The database writes confirmation and resolution users and times itself. Jobs, including agents working for a user, can propose but cannot decide. Confirmation stays when a different user later resolves the date. Dates, sources and decisions cannot be rewritten or removed; a correction is a new proposal and a reasoned drop.

Today's `critical_date` rows keep their dates, sources and notes. The [copy rules](#copying-todays-critical-dates) describe how kinds carry over. A date directly added by a user takes its confirmation from `created_by` and `created_at`; dates proposed by automation still need user confirmation. `cleared` maps to `done`. Resolved rows take their user, time and reason from the resolution event log; a legacy resolution without that evidence is listed for a partner to fix. The owner's load preserves known users and times with the transition and stamp triggers disabled, and lists missing evidence or decisions for a partner to fix rather than inventing confirmation. Today shows confirmed open dates; proposals stay out of Today and are read per deal as `critical_date_proposals`. `everything_about_deal` lists a deal's `open` dates as `critical_dates`; proposals and done, dropped or passed dates stay out. `critical_date_proposals` lists only that deal's `proposed` dates in the same read, by due date then id. Both lists contain the complete critical date row without `deal_id`, and return `[]` when empty. The old deal read lists dates per deal in that order (`carr-system` at `7a8ca32`, the `get-deal-room` tool and `v_deal_room_critical_date`). ## Marketing

The owner's automation keeps posting as it does today. These tables record what went out and how each post did, so each week's posts can learn from earlier ones.

- **`social_account`**: a presence on X, LinkedIn, Facebook or Instagram, by its handle, owned by a party. "Account" alone means a national account (`docs/glossary.md`). It holds the platform's own id for it, learned at sign-in, and `engine_publishes_from`, the time from which the engine publishes its posts.
- **`social_sign_in`**: one row each time a user signs in to a platform for an account: who, as which platform account, when, when it expires, and the scopes granted. The token itself lives in Workers KV, never in the database. A Facebook Page's sign-in does not expire, so it has no expiry.
- **`platform_format`**: the formats each platform takes, one row each: text, photo, photo set (several photos or a carousel), document (a PDF carousel, LinkedIn only) and video (a Reel on Instagram). Instagram takes no text-only post, and Facebook no photo set. X keeps its rows for the posts already sent there. Only a schema change adds one.
- **`idea`**: what a post says, as its angle, hook and pillar. One idea can run on several accounts.
- **`design_template`**: a reusable look that a post's image is made from.
- **`platform_metric`**: the metrics each platform reports, one row each. LinkedIn and X do not report saves, for example. The rows are a guess, to be confirmed against each platform's API. Each row says from when posts must have the metric. The first rows hold from the start, and a row added later holds from when it is added, so older posts, which have no reading of it, stay in the day-7 comparison. Only a schema change adds one.
- **`post`**: one post on one account, with its format, idea, design template, when it was scheduled, and its status. A post without an idea or a template still counts for its account.
- **`post_text`**: a post's words, one row per version. The weekly batch writes version 1 through intake. A user's edit is the next version, stamped with its user and time, so nothing is overwritten. The latest version is the one posted.
- **`post_media`**: a post's files in order, each a `document` in R2.
- **`post_review`**: what happened in review: a user's edit, naming its text version, a user's cancel, or no response, written when review closes on a post nobody touched.
- **`measurement`**: one reading of one metric of a post: when it was taken, its source and its value. A reading of a metric the post's platform does not report is refused, and so is a reading before the post is published. A reading that could not be taken is stored as not measured, with its reason, so a gap never reads as zero.

A post goes out unless a user cancels it. `scheduled` means the engine will publish it. At its time the publisher claims it by moving it to `publishing` with `where status = 'scheduled'`, so only one run can send it. It then becomes `published`, with the platform's id and time, or `failed`, with the platform's error as its reason. A post stuck in `publishing` is resolved by a user to one or the other, never sent again, and the post names that user. A cancelled post keeps its reason. Users edit or cancel until review closes, an hour before the post's time, and each edit or cancel is a review row. A post another publisher sent, such as Blotato, is stored as `published` or `failed` from the start.

One publisher holds each account at each minute. The database refuses a scheduled post, or a claim, before its account's `engine_publishes_from`, and an account without one never gets an engine post. The weekly batch sends earlier posts elsewhere. Moving the time later stops new claims at once, and the engine cancels the posts it no longer holds. The engine's role cannot set or change the time, even on an account it adds; the owner sets it at each cut-over.

Ratios are calculated when read, never stored. Engagement is the sum of the reactions, comments, shares and saves that the post's platform reported when it was published, and engagement per view divides it by views. After a metric is added, newer posts sum more metrics than older ones, so best and worst across that date lean toward newer posts.

`posts_at_day_7(as_of)` compares posts at the same age, because a post's numbers keep growing for days. Each post is judged by its latest reading of each metric at or before 168 hours after it was published. It returns, per account, how many posts it compared and the best and worst by engagement per view.

A post is left out while it is younger than 7 days, when it has no reading by day 7 of views or of an engagement metric its platform reports, or one whose latest reading by day 7 was not measured, or when it had no views.

Best and worst stay within one account, so they compare like with like. Averages per idea and design template come next (#206). They can mix platforms that count engagement differently, so each will show how many of its posts are on each platform.

Each ranked post carries `reading_age_hours`, the age of the oldest reading it was judged on. A post read only on day 3 shows 72, so the weekly loop and the Marketing screen can see it was judged early.

Pillars and hooks are text for now. They become lists, like roles and phases, with the rotation rules.

## Deals

A deal records what kind of transaction it is and why the client is taking space, each from its own short list. The owner ruled on #196 that today's one deal type is two facts, because a start-up lease was filed as one or the other (2026-11-16). The kinds are lease, purchase, sale-leaseback, build-to-suit and other. The reasons are start-up, relocation, expansion, renewal and additional office. Either may be unknown, as a deal's kind often is before a site is found.

Open deal names are unique across both partners and all workspaces. The database matches names without regard to case or surrounding whitespace. Closing, invoicing or losing a deal frees its name. Reopening checks the name again. Parking keeps the name in use.

Every deal has exactly one deal owner, a user, in `owner_user_id`. A second user can still be on the deal in another role. A new deal owner is an update of that column, so change history keeps who held the deal before, who moved it and when, with no dated table of its own. Who held a deal on a given day is the latest change to `owner_user_id` at or before it.

A deal is for one client, the business (owner, #594). The client's people, such as two doctors in one practice, are its contacts, never more clients, so `deal_party_one_client` refuses a second client on a deal. The rehearsal copy of 2026-11-17 has none. Five of its 71 deals have no client yet, so the rule is at most one. A deal copy run after today's system changed a deal's client lists the new client as `deal_has_another_client` instead of adding it.

Someone who leaves a deal in the new database is a removed `deal_party` row, and a new deal owner is a changed `owner_user_id`, so change history keeps both. Today's system keeps them as dated rows instead. It keeps when each person joined and left a deal, and closes one `lead` row and opens the next when the deal owner changes. Those earlier periods have their own tables, which only the owner's copy fills. `imported_deal_party` keeps a person or company, their role and the deal. `imported_deal_owner` keeps the user who owned the deal. Each row keeps the old row's id, when the period started and when it ended. A period ends after it starts. An empty start means the old row cannot say when it started.

The owner change events already copy into `imported_change`, with who moved the deal, when and why. An earlier owner period keeps only who held the deal and over what span, so the copy never turns a `lead` row into a change or an event into a period. Who held a deal before the copy is the period covering that time. When no period covers it, the owner change events are the evidence.

These tables hold what today's system recorded, not a complete history. A deal with no row may still have had earlier people, and an old start date can be when the row was stored rather than when the work began. Periods that cannot be placed are refused, and the copy lists them for correction in today's system:

- One person in one role on one deal holds one period at a time. Two periods that share a moment are refused. So are two periods with empty starts, since neither can be placed before the other.
- A deal has one earlier owner at a time.
- Someone back on a deal rejoined no earlier than their last period ended, so the current `deal_party` row's `created_at` is at or after that end. A merge that would break either rule is refused, as a merge of overlapping brand memberships is.

The engine reads these tables but cannot add, change or remove them, as with `imported_change`. A merge moves their parties to the survivor. A test proves each rule.

A deal names its market from the `market` list, which can be set when the deal opens, before any property is chosen, and stays empty until known. A closed deal records its value, such as the total lease value or the purchase price, in `closed_value`, which is empty until known and never zero. Nothing requires it at the move to closed, because the mail job closes a deal when its invoice arrives and the invoice does not carry it, so the value is left for a user to fill in.

A parked deal is not active work for now, and says why in `parking_reason`: the client paused, the prospect was never active, or another reason, with an optional note. No reason means the deal is active, as no disposition means a vendor is. Parking is not a phase and never changes one, so reviving a deal, which clears its reason and note, leaves it in the phase it was in. The deal keeps who last parked it and when in `parked_at`, `parked_by_user_id` and `parked_automatically`, apart from its last-change stamp. The database allows an automatic parker only on a parked deal with no user parker. Changing the parking reason or note stamps the acting user and clears the automatic attribution. Reviving a deal clears its parking time and attribution. Change history keeps every earlier parking and revival.

`needs_attention` is a flag a user sets on a deal and clears, never worked out from anything else. Change history keeps who set it and when. Parking, reviving and flagging are each a user's act, or an agent's working for one, so a job working for no one, such as the publisher, cannot do them; it can still change the deal's other columns. A copy of today's parked deals turns that check off, as it does the stamp, so it invents no acting user, and brings each one's own parking stamp instead (see below).

The Deals board groups a deal under a national account when its client is a current location of that account. The grouping comes from the client's dated brand membership.

Moving today's rows across:

- `deal_type` splits in two. `lease`, `purchase`, `sale_leaseback` and `build_to_suit` become the kind, with no reason. `relocation`, `expansion` and `additional_office` become the reason of the same slug, and `startup` the reason `start_up`, each with no kind until a user sets it. The live `renewal` reason requires a linked source lease. Ended deals with the old `renewal` type and an unrecorded source lease keep their historical Renewal label. `other` becomes the kind of the same slug, as the board shows it today.
- `deal.owner`, a partner's slug, becomes that partner's user as the deal owner. A closed or lost deal with none takes its open `deal_participant` row with role `lead`. A deal with neither is listed. Earlier deal owners, from the closed `lead` rows, come across into `imported_deal_owner` (see above). Ended participants come across into `imported_deal_party`.
- `deal.city` becomes the market whose name matches it, and a national deal's `deal_market_assignment.market` the same way. One that matches no market is listed for a user to add the market or choose one.
- `won_value` becomes `closed_value`. `sf_commission_placeholder` is not copied (see "Commissions").

Each run lists a deal whose stated lane disagrees with the copied account links as `lane_disagrees_with_account`: national means its client location held a brand membership in a national account on the Chicago date the deal opened. ## Markets and territories

A territory is the area someone serves. A user's territory, a vendor's market and a lead's area are one idea, so one `territory` table serves all three, and `party_territory` says who serves which, dated. A vendor that leaves a market keeps the old row, with its end date.

A territory is a set of pieces, because its shape varies. Each `territory_piece` is exactly one of:

- **a whole state**, by its two-digit Census FIPS code;
- **a named market**, a `market` row;
- **a distance from a base**, a latitude and longitude and how many miles the team will travel from it.

A named market covers whole counties, by their five-digit FIPS codes, in `market_county`. Counties are what metro areas are drawn from, so a market keeps the boundary the partners mean, and a property's county comes back with its point when its address is geocoded, such as by the Census geocoder. ZIP prefixes cross state and metro lines. A point and radius is already the distance piece. A property's state is the first two digits of its county.

`property_territory` lists each territory each property falls in. It has one branch per piece kind: the state of the property's county, a market holding its county, or a base within the piece's miles of its point. `miles_between` measures along the earth's surface. A property with no county or point falls in no territory until it is geocoded. Who serves a property is that list joined to `party_territory`.

The distance is plain SQL. PostGIS is offered on the production Neon project, but the check's PostgreSQL has no PostGIS, and changing the check is the owner's. `earthdistance` is in both, but as an extension it belongs to the whole database, not to the namespace this schema loads into. A sphere is close enough for "how far we will travel": the formula is the haversine, on a 3,958.8-mile radius.

A membership can carry what a reviewer found behind it, in `party_territory_review`: whether the party has an office in the territory, the coverage it declares in its own words, and where that came from. A review states an office, a coverage or both, and always its source. The coverage stays the words as written. Nothing draws it into pieces, so only the pieces decide which properties a territory covers. Who reviewed it and when is the user or job that last saved it, in its own `reviewed_by` and `reviewed_at` fields. Every save is a review, so a user confirming the same evidence becomes its reviewer, and change history keeps each earlier review. A review's date is never the membership's start, so a membership whose start is unknown keeps an empty `started_on`. A membership has at most one review, and a reviewed membership can be removed only after its review.

A merge moves each review with its membership and keeps its reviewer and time, because the merger reviewed nothing. Where the duplicate's membership and the survivor's, of one territory with one start, become one, the duplicate's review moves to the survivor's, and that move's change stamp and change history name the merger. Otherwise only the membership's party changes and the review row is untouched. If both are reviewed, the merge is refused until a user removes the wrong review, as a merge of overlapping brand memberships is refused.

Today's system keeps a vendor's markets as a list of named markets with a boundary written as text, a vendor's membership in each, and a free-text territory on the vendor. Moving them across, each old market becomes a `market` once its counties are listed, each membership a `party_territory` row on a territory holding that market, and each text territory a territory for a partner to draw. `copy-markets.sql` copies a membership's office presence, service coverage and source as its review, with its reviewer mapped by `copy_attribution` and its original review time. Old vendors that become one record can each hold a membership of one market. They copy to one membership, which takes their review only when every one agrees on office, coverage and source, including empty coverage. The review selection ranks merged-away vendors last, then vendors whose old party differs from the mapped party, as the vendor copy does. Within that rank the latest review supplies its reviewer and time, with vendor ID breaking a tie. Reviews that disagree wait listed for correction in today's system. A market's own source backs its boundary text, which comes across as counties, so it stays listed.

Rents and expenses read from LOIs and leases build the market database. It needs no table of its own. A round already holds its rent, NNN, TI and free rent, and names its property, so a market's rents are the rounds whose property falls in it, read with `property_territory` or `market_county`. A lease's terms join the same way when leases come. A figure read from a document outside any deal, such as a broker's survey, is a following slice, with its source document and date.

## Vendors

A vendor is a person or company holding the `vendor` role. The owner ruled on #257 that a vendor's category, such as lender or contractor, is a role (2026-11-15). Each category is a role whose `category_of` is vendor, and a vendor holds a `party_role` row for each one. On a deal, a vendor is a `deal_party` row in the role it played, such as lender, so the deal shows it under that role and its track record reads the same rows. A vendor nobody has categorized holds only the vendor role, which says so plainly, where a catch-all category would hide it. A rare category is a new role.

A vendor's `offers` and `seeking` are optional nonblank text on its vendor role. Practice types served are `vendor_vertical` rows, and loan programs are `vendor_loan_program` rows, each linked to that vendor role, one trimmed nonblank value per row. Today's schema and writer have no fixed vocabulary for either, so the new database invents none. Each row keeps its creation date, user or job stamp and change history. A user corrects a list by removing an entry and adding another. A merge fills missing offers and seeking from the duplicate, keeps the survivor's nonempty text and unions both lists (decision 71).

A vendor's stage and relationship level are on its vendor role, because they describe the team's relationship with the vendor, not one of its categories. A stage comes from its role's own list in `stage`, so leads can take their own stages on the same column later. The four levels, as the owner defined them for today's system:

| Level | Label       | Meaning                                                                      |
| ----- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0     | Prospective | Worth knowing. No two-way contact yet.                                       |
| 1     | Building    | Two-way contact: they replied, or we met. Reaching out alone does not count. |
| 2     | Established | Value has moved in either direction: an introduction, a referral or a deal.  |
| 3     | Core        | Value has moved both ways, more than once.                                   |

No level means not yet judged, a different fact from 0.

A vendor's relationship partner is optional and lives on its vendor role as `party_role.owner_user_id`. It uses the same reference to `app_user` as a lead's owner because both name the user responsible for that role. Vendor check-in cadence reads this partner.

A vendor's disposition is the team's ruling on it, and its target flag says the team wants to meet it. Both sit on the vendor role next to its stage and level (owner, #258). A disposition is parked, deliberately dormant and on drip only, or avoid, ruled out as a partner. No disposition is active, in the network. A ruling always carries its reason, so a vendor never leaves play without one. An avoided vendor cannot be a target. Disposition and target are not stages: today's stage list once held them, and the old system's migration 0047 split them out because a stage mixed how well the team knows a vendor, a ruling and an intent.

An introduction is one `introduction` row, made either way: the vendor introducing someone to the team, such as a new client, or the team introducing someone to the vendor. It names the vendor, who made it, whom it introduced, when, a note, and the deal it led to, which can be set when one comes of it. One the team made names the user who made it.

`vendor_track_record` gives each vendor's record from deal history only. Nothing stores a rating (owner, #257). It counts the deals the vendor worked in any vendor role, how many of them closed and how many died, by each deal's latest phase, and the deals that its introductions to the team, and the team's to it, led to. A deal counts once, however many roles or introductions tie it to the vendor.

Moving today's rows across:

- Each `vendor` becomes the vendor role on its party, with its `relationship_level`, `disposition` and `is_target` as they are, and `active` becomes no disposition. These columns hold 0047's split and every change since, so they win over an old stage. A parked or avoided vendor with no recorded reason keeps the ruling with `not recorded, set before the new system` (decision 176).
- Each `vendor_category` row becomes a role whose `category_of` is vendor, and a vendor's `category_slug` its role. The deal roles of `deal_vendor_assignment`, such as title and inspector, join the same list, and `gc` becomes contractor. Each assignment becomes a `deal_party` row, and so does each worked deal in a vendor's `deal_evidence`.
- Each `vendor_stage` row becomes a vendor stage, and a vendor's `stage` the stage on its vendor role, except the nine original stages. The old system's migration 0047 split those into level, disposition and target, which already hold what they meant, so none of them, such as avoid or target, comes back as a stage (owner, #258).
- Each `party_link` of kind `introduced` or `referred`, or of the older `intro`, `intro_received` or `referral`, becomes an introduction. One whose `via_party` is the vendor was made by the vendor, and one made through a user by the team, naming that user. One whose `via_party` is empty, or is neither the vendor nor a user, waits for a partner to say who made it. Each `party_link_deal` whose `referred_by` is a vendor becomes an introduction made by that vendor, with its deal. A `can_introduce` or `intro_requested` link has not happened, so it is not an introduction (see "Who knows whom").
- `trust_override`, a hand-set rating, does not move.

## Who knows whom

The owner ruled on #196 that "knows" and "can introduce" links between people are kept, as dated links between two parties (2026-11-16). Each is one `party_link` row of kind `knows` or `can_introduce`, between any two people or companies, with an optional `started_on`, `ended_on` and note. Knowing goes both ways, so a knows link is stored once whichever party it names first, and "who knows Avery" reads both ends. Can introduce has a direction: `party_id` can introduce the team to `other_party_id`. A link that lapses keeps its row with its end date, and a later one is a new row.

A can-introduce link and an introduction are different facts. The link says someone could make an introduction, between any two parties. An introduction says one was made, with a vendor on one side, either way, and which deal it led to. When an offered introduction happens with a vendor, it becomes an `introduction` row, and the link can be ended; nothing joins the two automatically, and neither counts toward a vendor's track record but the introduction.

Moving today's `party_link` rows across:

- `knows` becomes a knows link from `from_party` to `to_party`, with `occurred_on` as its start, its note and its author.
- `can_introduce` with no `via_party` becomes a can-introduce link from `from_party` to `to_party`. With a `via_party`, the broker can introduce the two ends to each other. When one end is a user, it becomes a link from the broker to the other end; otherwise it is listed for a partner.
- `works_with` and `intro_requested` are outside the ruling, so each is listed for a partner.
- `introduced`, `referred` and the older `intro`, `intro_received` and `referral` follow "Vendors" above. One with no vendor on either side has no place and is listed.
- `source` does not move.

## Leads and clients

A lead is a person or company holding the `lead` role, and a client one holding the `client` role, so a lead that becomes a client is the same record with one more role. The owner ruled on #196 (walkthrough 4, 2026-11-16).

- **Becoming a client.** Someone is a client as soon as a deal is opened for them, signed or not. The client on a deal is a `deal_party` row, and the database refuses one whose party does not hold the client role. The engine adds the role with the deal, in one save, and the role's `created_at` is when they became a client. The lead role stays, with its stage, owner and history, so the Leads screen can leave out a lead who has become a client.
- **Owner.** A lead's owner is the user who works it. None means nobody works it yet, and any user can take it. Territory is shared, so a lead's area never picks its owner. Change history keeps who owned it before.
- **Stage.** A lead's stage comes from the lead stages in `stage`: New, Qualified, Outreach Active, Engaged, Nurture, Opportunity and Archived. Nurture replaces named drip campaigns. Do not contact is the party's `do_not_contact` row, and deal outcomes are deal phases, not lead stages.
- **How found.** Each lead and client says how they were found. The `how_found` choices are referral, vendor, marketing post, public records, cold outreach, own research, inbound, national account or corporate lead, existing relationship, and event. Not recorded is only for records copied from the old system with no source, and a recorded source replaces it on a later copy (owner, #604). It is a fact about the person or company, so it holds in every role, and a lead that becomes a client copies nothing. A list can be counted for lead attribution, where free text cannot.
- **Stage evidence.** `lead_stage_move` keeps applied evidence, with the activity, reference and strength. Pending weak moves use the shared `proposal` queue below. Applying evidence holds the lead and changes its stage only if it still matches `from_stage`. Unattended jobs apply strong evidence only along New → Qualified → Outreach Active → Engaged → Nurture or Opportunity. Weak evidence and archiving require a direct user, and archiving a nonblank reason. Ordinary user stage edits use change history. Applied evidence is immutable; undo is a new move or correction. Matching suggestions and evidence collapse on merge, with removed rows retained in change history.
- **Working details.** The lead role keeps its `lane` from `lead_lane`, `segment`, and `estimated_lease_on` with `lease_estimate_source` and optional high, medium or low confidence. An estimate always has its source; neither can stand alone. Lanes are independent of how found. `party.how_found_detail` keeps the attribution detail in every role. A merge keeps the survivor's working details, filling gaps from the duplicate, and moves an estimate with its own source and confidence. How-found detail fills only when the source agrees or the survivor had none.

- **Outreach drafts.** `lead_contact_draft` keeps one draft per lead, its subject, body, owner, intended send time and time zone. `requires_human_send` is always true and `dispatchable` always false. Approval never sends. The engine inserts only draft content, so approval and revision take their defaults. A user or an agent working for one may edit or approve only while the lead is qualified and has no active do-not-contact restriction. Any edit to the text, owner or intended time clears approval and increments the database-written revision. A caller approves only with the revision it read in its update condition. Approval actors and times stay in change history. A merge keeps the survivor's draft and retains the removed duplicate's text in change history.

- **Candidates.** `candidate` is the separate list from sweeps and public records. Its source and source key identify a row once, with its original object-shaped context, name and qualifying reason. Unreviewed rows name no party. A user, including an agent working for one, promotes to a party holding the lead role, or declines with a reason. Jobs may suppress with a reason; a user must reopen before promoting. Promoted and declined decisions stay decided, so sweeps cannot put them back in the list. Only a merge changes the promoted party. Source and source key never change. Sweeps may refresh source context, name, qualification and estimates only while unreviewed; change history keeps earlier facts. Decline reasons stay as the user decided, and candidates are never removed.
- **Not kept.** The 0 to 100 lead score and named drip campaigns do not come across (owner, #196).

Moving today's rows across:

- Each `lead` becomes the lead role on its party, and each `client` the client role on its party, keeping its `created_at`. A client merged away today is not copied. A party with several leads or clients keeps the earliest `created_at` and the stage and owner of its most recently changed lead, and is listed for a partner.
- Stages `new`, `qualified`, `outreach_active`, `engaged`, `opportunity` and `archived` stay. `nurture_drip` and `closed_lost` become nurture, as the Leads screen already shows Closed-Lost. `active_deal` and `closed_won` become archived with the reason "became a deal in the old system" in stage history. Archiving keeps it out of Prospects even when the deal link did not survive the copy. A `do_not_contact` stage or `suppressed` lead becomes archived under the existing contact restriction rule. Today that sets only the lead, not the person's `contact_state`, so the lead copy needs a partner-reviewed reason to create its `do_not_contact` row. Without a reviewed reason it lists the restriction for correction.
- `owner_id` maps through `outside_id` to an existing `app_user`. Leads keep `party_role.owner_user_id`. A lead owned by automation or a system actor copies unclaimed. Clients keep nullable `client.owner_user_id`, separate from national-account ownership. For a client owned by automation or a system actor, all its old deals must name the same mapped user. A reviewed client owner takes precedence. The copy lists unresolved owners, label-only owners without reviewed input, conflicting owners on clients mapped to one party, and an existing different client owner. It retries missing client owners without overwriting existing owners or changing stamps. A record with neither an ID nor a label keeps no owner unless the owner supplies a reviewed client owner.
- How found comes from `client.acquisition_source`, asked of the client, or else `lead.source_type`. Both are free text today. A `prospect-pool` lead is mapped by its candidate's own pool source. Every other value is listed for a partner to map to the list, and so is a party whose two values disagree. A lead or client with neither waits, listed, since the database refuses one without how it was found.
- A lead whose `client_id` points at a client on another party, such as the practice a doctor runs, keeps its lead role on the person, and the client role stays on the practice.
- `score`, `score_reason`, `drip_campaign`, `drip_added`, `report_back_due` and `sf_deal` do not move. Only the old spreadsheet import wrote the last four, and nothing reads them.
- Each applied old `lead_stage_move` becomes evidence on its lead party; a pending one becomes a shared proposal, keeping its id, activity, evidence reference, strength and reason. `nurture_drip` becomes `nurture`. Proposal creation and approval map to their respective change-history entries, retaining the old actors and dates. An undone row remains applied evidence followed by its undo in change history. A missing activity, blank evidence or reason, unknown actor or contradictory stage is listed for a user rather than invented. The owner loads historical rows without replaying the application trigger; ordinary stage events map to change history only. Run `schema/copy-lead-stage-moves.sql` in its own owner transaction after the lead and activity copies. Existing evidence and proposals stay unchanged; conflicting source changes are listed. Undo and ordinary stage events become role stage changes in history, with the recorded actor and time. Matching keys, stage values and stamps make reruns add no history.

- `est_lease_event`, `event_source` and `event_confidence` become the estimate date, source and confidence. A promoted candidate's `est_basis` supplies a missing source. An estimate without a source, confidence without an estimate, unknown confidence or an unparsed `est_lease_event_raw` is listed rather than inferred.

- `notes` becomes a note activity about the party, dated at the old row's last change. `last_touch` comes from the latest contact activity; a standalone old date or imported last-touch-stamp note with no contact activity is listed rather than invented as contact. `notes_path` is an old file reference, not note text; its file must be copied as a document or listed for a user.

It ends as the deal next-step copy describes, never at the row's last change: done at its completion event or with no time, cancelled with its recorded reason or the approved import wording, and expired with its Clear the deck event when that proves it. So is `prospect_engagement_status` on a prospect or an inactive client; elsewhere it holds only its default.
- Each old `lead_contact_draft` becomes a draft on its lead party, preserving id, subject, body, owner, schedule and time zone. The old revision and approval become its revision and approved flag, with old creation and approval actors and times in change history. ## Suggestions from mail, calls and meetings

`proposal` is the pending decision queue for a next step, deal phase move, new lead role on a known party, or weak lead-stage move. Typed columns and foreign keys constrain each payload. A source and payload are stored once, and a party suggestion requires an activity about that party. Stage suggestions retain their evidence strength. The source is an email, call or meeting activity with nonblank evidence. The originating job is retained independently of the current stamp. Only a direct user accepts or dismisses, once, and the database writes that user's id and decision time. The shared change history retains the original job and decision. Payloads and final decisions cannot be rewritten or removed.

Acceptance creates the real next step, phase history row, lead role or applied stage-evidence row in the same transaction. A failure leaves the proposal pending. Next steps and applied stage evidence use the proposal's id. Phase moves check the expected current phase while holding the deal, and stage moves use the existing conditional lead update. A new lead requires an existing party, starts New and supplies how found if the party lacks it. Dismissal creates nothing. Corrections are new proposals.

The owner added the "log a call" suggestion kind on 2026-11-18. A `log_call` suggestion keeps a summary and cites the recorded call about its deal or party. Acceptance saves a note on that target with the proposal's id, the cited call's time and the suggested summary. The cited call remains the only call activity (decision 151).

Today's `email_proposal` becomes this queue, with pending, confirmed and dismissed mapped to pending, accepted and dismissed. Its email activity, quote, text, due date and actors carry across; a phase proposal without its exact target waits for a user rather than inventing one. `capture_candidate` and `capture_post_call_candidate` next steps and lead suggestions use the same queue; confirmed `capture_post_call_action` rows become real next steps linked by id. Reports and session state in `capture_session`, `capture_post_call_report` and `phone_call_session` become source activity summaries and evidence references, without a second review workflow. `ops.meeting` and its notes become meeting activities; actions and revisions map to proposals and change history. Decision requests map to decisions only when a user actually decided. Meeting mode, its stream and processing leases are dropped. Reply drafts are neither suggestions nor outreach drafts; see "Reply drafts". `email_lead_exclusion` holds only a hash of the intake's local mailbox key for mail its model read as not business, so no email, sender or user decision can be found for it; each is listed and none becomes a suggestion. Historical accepted suggestions load with their existing real rows, without replaying acceptance. Missing source, job, payload or decision provenance is listed for a user, never fabricated. This slice defines that mapping; it does not copy production rows.

## Merging duplicates

The owner ruled on #196: the merged-away record disappears and everything points at the survivor. `merge_party(duplicate, survivor)` leaves a `merged_party` row, so an old link finds the survivor, moves every row that points at the duplicate, then removes it. Email matches need nothing more, because the duplicate's addresses are now the survivor's. The foreign keys then refuse any row that still names the duplicate, in any table, including tables added later. The merge finds what to move from the catalog, so a new table that points at a party moves with no change to it.

A conflict keeps the survivor's data and refuses what cannot be settled safely:

- Two people, two companies, or a person and a company? Only two of one kind merge.
- A user? A user is never merged away, because their changes name them. They can be the survivor.
- Both have an NPI? Refused. NPPES issues one number per person or organization, so two numbers are two of them. Otherwise the survivor keeps its own NPI or takes the duplicate's.
- The same email, phone number, role, specialty, company link, territory link or activity on both? One row stays, the survivor's, with its label and title.
- Both have a primary email or phone? The survivor's stays primary. If only the duplicate has one, it becomes the survivor's.
- Do not contact on either? The survivor is do not contact. If both are, the longer restriction stays with its reason, with no end date stronger than either date. Equal end dates keep the survivor's reason.
- Both on one deal in the same role? One link stays, and the duplicate's rounds move to it. In different roles, both stay.
- The survivor keeps its name. Outside ids, next steps, sent activity, social accounts and introductions all move.
- A knows or can-introduce link? It moves. One between the two records is dropped, and where both hold the same link, either way round for knows, the survivor's stays.
- Both leads? The survivor's lead role stays, with its stage and owner, and the duplicate's owner fills a missing one. Needs attention stays set if either lead had it. How found stays the survivor's, or becomes the duplicate's.
- Both vendors? The survivor's vendor role stays, with its stage, level and relationship partner. The duplicate's values fill any it lacks. The stricter disposition stays with its reason: avoid, then parked, then the survivor's. If either is a target, the survivor is, unless the merged disposition is avoid.
- A vendor and someone it introduced, or was introduced to? Refused, because an introduction joins two different records.
- A record merged into the duplicate earlier now points at the survivor, so pointers never chain.

A catalog test lists every unique key that holds a party. A new one fails it until the merge says which row stays.

## Change history

Every insert, update and delete of a stamped row writes one `change` row: the table, the record's key, whether the row was added, changed or removed, and the stamp of who made it and when. An added row's `new_value` is the whole row, and a removed row's `old_value` is the whole row. A changed row's hold only the fields it changed, from what to what. The stamp's own columns are left out, because the change's stamp names who and when. A statement that changes nothing writes nothing.

When a weekly review mark moves to a deal, both sides of its history entry include the week, so both items' mark versions advance. The entry names the partner who converted the prospect. The mark itself keeps its original stamp.

The key is the row's primary key, such as `{"id": …}` for a deal, or `{"deal_id": …, "party_id": …, "role": …}` for someone on a deal. `activity_about` and `post_review` have none, so their key is the whole row. Who moved a deal to which phase, and when, is the deal's `deal_phase_change` inserts: `table_name = 'deal_phase_change' and record ->> 'deal_id' = <deal id>`.

One trigger function, `record_change`, writes history for every table that carries the stamp, and a test fails when a stamped table has no history. It runs with the owner's rights, and no role but the owner may attach it to a table, so a temporary table cannot write history either. The engine's role reads `change` but cannot add, change or remove a row of it, so history is written only by changing a record.

A merge marks each change it makes with the duplicate it merged, in `merge_of`: each link it moves or removes, the duplicate's removal and its merged-into pointer. `merge_party` sets `engine.merge` for its own run, and the mark counts only when a merge of that record was saved in the same transaction, so setting it by hand cannot pass an edit off as an earlier merge's. Set by hand after a merge in the same transaction, it would mark an unrelated edit as that merge's; the change still names who made it and when.

Today's system keeps one `event` row per field changed, with the actor, a verb and a cause, and `/pipeline/changes` reads a deal's events. A change row holds every field one statement changed, so the old per-field rows are `jsonb_each` over its values. A copy of old rows, such as `schema/copy-parties.sql`, keeps each row's old stamp, and its history records the copy itself: an insert by `old_system`, at the time the copy ran. The old `event` rows come across separately as `imported_change`, described below. Serving the change feed and deal events from this table is a later slice.

Only the schema owner's database role may call `erase_change(removed_change_id, fields)`. It requires a delete change and refuses a record that exists again. It removes the selected non-key text fields from old and new values for that table and record, across inserts, edits and deletions. Unknown fields, keys and non-text fields are refused. Other values stay. Keys, actions, merge pointers, authors and times stay unchanged. An `erase` change records the request with its user or job and time, with the erased field names and without copying their words. Repeating a completed erase changes nothing. The function locks the source table while it checks absence and erases, so a concurrent insert cannot recreate the record during it. The engine cannot call it or change history directly. Linked records and external copies are separate requests.

## Commissions

The owner answered walkthrough 4, topic E, on #196 (2026-11-16). A deal's gross commission, what the brokerage collects, is recorded once, on its `commission` row. An open deal can carry its expected commission there too, so the pipeline has a total. A gross is paid in parts, one `commission_payment` each, with its invoiced, due and received dates. Whether a payment is invoiced, unpaid, overdue or paid is read from those dates and today's date, never stored.

The split pays others first. Each `commission_share` gives a referring agent, the brokerage or anyone else a share of the whole. The two users split what is left. The closing user is the deal's owner. Their ladder, the `commission_tier` rows keyed by that user, gives their part by their lifetime count of closed deals, and their partner, named on the ladder, takes the rest. The count never resets: a deal is the user's nth closed deal if it is the nth they own whose latest phase is closed, by when it closed. A tier runs from its count until the next tier starts, so tiers cannot overlap or leave a gap. A single deal overrides its users' split with its own closing part. The shares and thresholds are the partners' rows, and real ones never appear in this repo, its tests or its docs.

`commission_split` works out everyone's share of each deal and its amount, so no user's take is typed. Each amount is the cents its running share of the whole adds, taking the others first and the partner last, so no amount is negative and together they are the gross. A deal that is not closed yet has no count, so its users' shares wait for it to close unless it overrides them. A gross is refused until its closing user has a ladder, because the ladder names the partner, even for a deal that overrides its closing part. The count and the ladder are read when asked, so a deal that closes, dies or changes owner moves the count for that owner's later deals, and a changed tier changes every deal it covers.

Moving today's rows across:

- Each deal's `commission` rows become one `commission` row. A row with status `expected` adds its `gross_amount` to the expected commission. Every other row becomes a payment of its `gross_amount`, with its `invoiced_on`, `due_on` and `received_on`, and the payments add up to the gross. A `received` row with no `received_on` is listed for a user to date.
- `deal.invoiced_on` becomes `imported_deal_invoiced`, which only the owner's copy fills, so a deal invoiced without a commission row stays invoiced. The invoice-email reconciliation below also moves it onto a payment it identifies. It is never a second editable date.
- `commission_allocation` has no rows, so nothing comes across. Its `referral_fee`, `house` and `other` kinds would be the referral, brokerage and other shares, and `partner_split` is the ladder.
- `sf_commission_placeholder` is not copied. Today's rule already keeps it out of every money figure.
- Today's fixed share per lane, in a report script, is replaced by each user's ladder. The partners enter their ladders themselves.

## Space needs, suites and building owners

`space_need` holds a deal's named requirement set with optional minimum and maximum square feet, a nonnegative finite yearly rent ceiling per square foot, space type, market and must-haves. Unknown bounds stay null, and a one-sided range is allowed. A deal can have several sets for alternative searches. Space type holds one type. Searches with several types wait for correction rather than being flattened into one. A vendor-backed need also keeps vendor_id, source_ref, source_revision and applicability together, all present or all absent. The engine can correct this provenance as a unit. The market uses the existing market list. Needs describe the client's requirements; `deal_property` continues to name candidate buildings.

`suite` is space within one `property`, with its label, floor, square feet, area basis and condition. Unknown suite labels remain null and are not treated as duplicates. Labels are unique within a building, so the same label can occur in another. Area basis keeps today's rentable, usable, county_heated and listed_unverified meanings. Area does not imply availability. `lease_suite` joins each signed lease version to zero or more suites by both suite and property. A composite key binds lease_version_id to lease_id. The lease and suite foreign keys both carry property_id, so every suite is in that version's lease identity's building. These stamped links have change history and cannot be rewritten or removed by the engine; a correction is another lease version.

`building_ownership` links a property to a person or company with start and end dates and a source. It retains today's owner, landlord_rep, property_manager and listing_agent kinds. Dates are inclusive, may be unknown and allow joint ownership. It does not pick a single owner for a building. Ending an ownership leaves its row; change history records corrections. A party merge moves these links and keeps the survivor's row when both hold the same property, kind and start.

The needs, suites and ownership tables have the shared user or job stamp and change history. The engine may correct their descriptive fields and ownership dates, and remove mistakes. Their deal, property and party links never change through ordinary edits, except that a space need's vendor provenance, including its vendor, can be corrected as a unit. A referenced suite stays while a lease points to it. This is schema design only; no production migration or search endpoint is added.

## Deals reads for the app

Steps 13–15 and 29 use `GET /api/engine/deals` and `GET /api/engine/deals/{deal_id}`. The board accepts `workspace` (`all`, `team`, or `national_account`) and `account_client_id`. It returns the signed-in partner's slug, open, closed and lost lists, and national account summaries. The detail returns the deal header, steps, checklist items, critical dates, files, lease, and history under the app's existing section names. The new detail route takes the deal UUID the board supplies.

Each new-schema board row and deal detail returns separate `kind` and `reason` slugs, with null for either unknown fact. The detail also returns `renews_lease_id` as the source lease UUID or null. The legacy `type` still uses the kind when known and otherwise uses the reason with its legacy spelling. An ended imported Renewal with an unrecorded source lease returns `reason: 'renewal'` and `renewal_lease_unrecorded: true` until it has a live reason. `parked_automatically: true` means the old automation parked the deal, and `parked_by` is a user slug or null. `next_critical_date` is `{ id, kind, due_on }` for the earliest confirmed date still open, or null when none exists. `due_on` is a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` format. Open dates that are already late still qualify.

Each national account's `overdue_deals` counts its active open deals with at least one open ordinary next step due before today in Central time. A retained date from a completed step and a checklist item's due date do not qualify.

The detail sends `checklist` as `deal-checklist.v1`. The template lives in engine code and needs no doctrine hash check. Its labels use sentence case and display as sent. Saved deal items and their due dates still come from `next_step`. The template shows progress from `current_lease` and its source file, the deal's invoice date, and commission payments. A copied renewal without a source lease shows the common checklist until a partner links the source lease. Commission received is complete when the deal has at least one saved payment and each payment has a receipt date. The expected amount is a forecast and has no effect on receipt progress. Confirmed open critical dates count as due or overdue by the Central calendar day. Document requests stay on the old side because Pass D found no rows to move (#718).

Both partners' detail reads return every file filed to the deal, including earlier versions. Each `documents` entry gives `id`, `name`, `kind`, `note`, `version`, `bytes`, `content_type` and `prepared_at`. Open the file by its `id` through `GET /api/engine/documents/{id}` (door 20).

The new detail read returns `correspondence`, an array of email messages and meetings linked directly to the deal by `activity_about`, newest first by `happened_at`. A filed email appears even when it has a pending follow-up. An unfiled email with a pending filing suggestion appears in `email_follow_ups`. Each correspondence entry gives `activity_id`, `kind`, and `happened_at`. An `email` also gives `sender`, `sender_name`, and `subject` from the stored message. Messages in the same Outlook conversation have their own entries. A `meeting` gives `title` from the part of the stored activity summary before the first blank line. The meeting notes copy puts the recorded title there. Other meetings use the saved summary caption, and an absent or blank caption returns null. Deal notes still come from `thread`. The old-schema read supplies `email_threads`.

Both partners' open board cards and deal pages return `client_portal_url` for an active registration belonging to the deal's current client. It is the site's address on `share.doctorcre.com`, or null when there is no matching active registration. The reader database role can read the fields used to form the link.

### Set up a client website

`POST /api/engine/deals/{deal_id}/client-website` is a signed-in partner save. It takes `idempotency_key`, `slug` (1 to 63 lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, starting with a letter or digit), and `password` (1 to 1,024 characters). It returns `200 { ok, deal_id, client_portal_url, created }`. A repeat with the same name and password and a fresh key returns `created: false`. A transport retry with the same key returns the original answer and `replayed: true`. The receipt fingerprint uses the deal and name, omitting the password as the old save did.

The save refuses a name reserved by another registration (`409 portal_slug_taken`), a different password for the same registration (`409 portal_password_differs`), or a new name for a deal with an active registration (`409 portal_deal_already_registered`). A missing deal or current client returns `404 portal_deal_not_found`. The database stores a bcrypt cost-12 verifier of the hex SHA-256 password digest, compatible with the copied registration. Setup registers the website's address and credential. Publishing the website remains a separate operation.

### Open a client website

`POST /api/engine/deals/{deal_id}/client-website/open` uses the signed-in session and CSRF token and returns `200 { url }` for the new tab. Either partner can request it. A deal with no active registration for its current client returns 404. The URL uses the engine's configured `CLIENT_WEBSITE_ORIGIN`. Without a valid HTTPS origin the door returns 503 `client_website_origin_unconfigured` before it issues a grant. The one-use grant expires after 60 seconds. The engine stores only its digest, tied to the website and signed-in user.

The share Worker calls the engine's private `ClientWebsiteAccess` service binding. `redeemGrant(slug, grant)` rechecks the current registration and consumes the matching grant atomically. The share Worker starts its own secure site session and redirects to the address with the grant removed. `verifyPassword(slug, password)` checks the same registration's stored verifier. The fifth failed password locks that website's password check for the rest of its 15-minute window. A correct password clears the failure count. The share Worker still owns the session cookie and the asset-decryption key.

Each `email_follow_ups` entry is one pending email suggestion targeting the deal, from either partner's mailbox. It gives `proposal_id`, `kind`, `deal_id`, `evidence`, the proposed step fields (`what`, `owner`, `due_on`) or phase fields (`from_phase`, `to_phase`), and an `email` with `activity_id`, `sender`, `sender_name`, `subject`, `happened_at` and `summary`. The `owner` field gives the app user slug. A filing suggestion drops from this list once the email is on the deal. Use the proposal ID to accept or dismiss through door 21. Accepting a filing suggestion files the email.

To return the app to CARR, set the KV value to `off`.

The deal page returns `imported_deal_history` in ascending `recorded_at` and `id` order, or `[]` when empty. Each entry has `id` (the source event UUID), `recorded_at`, `occurred_at`, `actor` (a user or job slug), `verb`, nullable `field`, `old_value`, `new_value`, and `imported: true`. Each value is null or an object with the recorded facts for that verb and field. Missing values are omitted. An explicit clear of a previously recorded fact keeps a null in `new_value`. Website links, research source URLs, internal paths, and next-action routing summaries stay in the source record. Use `verb` and `field` to label what happened. In imported deal openings, the old `deal_type` values `relocation`, `startup`, `additional_office`, `expansion`, and `renewal` appear under `reason` for the Deal reason label. A next step also gives its assigned `owner`. An open row in `next_actions` gives its latest `change_id`, or zero if there is no recorded change. An ended row gives `null`.

Reply drafts are visible to their owner while pending, sending, or failed. One active draft appears for each known Outlook conversation. An approved or failed draft in this group travels on the suggestion card as `fallback_reply_draft: { id, version, status, failure_reason }`. Sent replies still show in Done. The working card's `base_version` accounts for both the draft and the suggestions while both are on it.

### Mail priorities

`GET /api/engine/home/mail-priorities` uses the same Home switch, signed-in partner and read-only database role. It takes no query parameters. ### What every door shares

- **Two entrances.** The app and a partner's own calls use `/api/engine/...` with the partner's session, so the save is stamped with that user. Studio jobs use `/jobs/...`, behind one Cloudflare Access service token for the Studio, like `/intake/*` (decision 106), and name themselves in two headers: `Engine-Job: <job slug>` and, when the job works for a partner, `Engine-For-User: <user slug>`. `runNextSave` turns either into its `Stamp`. A door listed under one entrance only refuses the other with 404.
- **Saves.** Every save is a JSON `POST` with `idempotency_key`, a UUID the caller makes once per intended change and reuses on every retry, for example a UUIDv5 of the source and the change. A retry gets the first answer back with `replayed: true` and changes nothing. The same key with other arguments or another stamp is `409 key_reuse`.
- **Refusals.** A refusal is `{ error, correlation_id, hint, ...details }`. Every door can answer `400 invalid_json`, `400 missing_required { fields }`, `400 invalid_field_value { fields }`, `401 unauthorized`, `403 unknown_job`, `422 actor_not_provisioned`, `422 database_refused_the_statement { rule }` with the constraint's name when a database rule refuses, and `503 database_unavailable`. A `5xx` is retried with the same key; a `4xx` is not, except where a door says so.
- **Ids.** Deals and activities keep their old ids in the copy, so ids a caller already holds still work. Parties, people and users are named by new ids or, for a user, by `app_user.slug`.
- **Timestamps.** Invoice capture, next-step completion, email lead suggestions, reply delivery and calendar agenda refuse impossible dates, year `0000` and offsets outside PostgreSQL's range with `400 invalid_field_value`.

Jobs that stamp these saves: `mail_capture` for intake, the router and the outbox, each working for the mailbox's partner; `lead_stage` for the nightly lead stages; `cadence` and `call_capture` for the nightly cadence and call capture, which the owner adds (see the list at the end); `agent` for the assistant, working for its partner.

Storing it leaves the step open.

| `POST /jobs/reply-drafts/{id}/delivery`                 | `mail_capture` for that partner          | `idempotency_key`, `version`, `sha256`, `status` (`sent` or `failed`), `sent_item_id` and `sent_at` when sent, `reason` when failed | `{ draft_id, version, status }`                                                                                                                         |

- **One draft per email.** A second store for an email that already has a draft for that partner answers the first draft unchanged, whatever its key, as today's store keeps one per email (`email-proposals.js:806`). The store inserts with `on conflict (email_activity_id, owner_user_id) do nothing` and reads the stored draft. A reply recheck with new text therefore never makes a second draft that could also be approved.
- **One open draft per conversation after a new store.** When a store creates a draft, only the draft for the newest email stays pending for that partner and Outlook conversation id. The newest email has the latest recorded email time (`activity.happened_at`), with the greatest activity UUID breaking ties. A draft stored late for an older email is dismissed in the same save. The save records dismissals with the reason and job in change history. Approved, sent, and failed drafts keep their status. Emails without a conversation id are independent. Home skips `reply_draft` rows with `status = 'dismissed'`, so a cleared draft no longer appears.
- **Never twice.** A draft is sent at one version, its `revision`, which every change its partner makes advances, approval included, and a recorded try never does. The outbox keys its send and receipt on draft id and version, as today, and checks the Sent folder before sending anything the list still shows. The engine records one `sent` per version: the same `sent_item_id` again converges, a `failed` whose reason matches `unconfirmed_after_` may later become `sent` when the outbox finds it in the Sent folder (`outlook_outbox.py:223`), and `sent` never becomes `failed`. A failed draft returns to the approved list only when its partner submits it again after `aborted_before_dispatch`, and any other failure stays failed, so it is never sent again on its own. The sent mail itself arrives through door 19.
- **Digest.** `sha256` is the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the UTF-8 bytes of `JSON.stringify({ subject, body })`, subject first, with nothing else in it. That is the same as `json.dumps({'subject': subject, 'body': body}, separators=(',', ':'), ensure_ascii=False)`, which the outbox recomputes and checks before sending anything (`outlook_outbox.py:34`). No column holds it: the door computes it from the approved subject and body, which a try cannot change.
- **Checks.** Delivery checks `version` and `sha256` against the approved draft and returns `409 draft_changed { version }` if the draft changed. A different sent item returns `409 already_sent { sent_item_id }`. A submission for a dismissed draft returns `409 draft_changed { version }`. Other stale submissions return `409 version_conflict { version }`. A partner who does not own the draft gets `403 not_mailbox_owner`. Attachments are documents, linked in `reply_draft_document` and fetched through door 20.

#### Open, save, or decline a reply draft from Home

- `POST /api/engine/reply-drafts/{id}/edit` requires `idempotency_key`, `version`, `subject`, and `body`. It saves the text and leaves the draft pending.

- All IDs are UUIDs. `party_id` names the existing person or company and `role` is its role slug on this deal. Each save adds or removes exactly that link. A missing link on removal returns `404 not_found`. The database records the removal in deal history. Deal ownership changes use door 16.
- A date the partner adds opens in the same save, with the database recording that partner and the confirmation time. `kind` is `loi_expiry`, `option_notice`, `lease_commencement`, `rent_start`, `lease_end`, `due_diligence`, `phase_effective_date` or `other`. `due_on` is a valid `YYYY-MM-DD` date, `source` is nonblank text, and `note` is optional nonblank text or null. ### 24. Home marks saved

`POST /api/engine/home/marks` saves one card's mark for the signed-in partner. Every request includes `idempotency_key` and the card's `item_id` and `base_version` from the new Home inbox read. Item IDs are `next_action:<UUID>`, `capture_post_call_action:<UUID>`, `notification:<UUID>`, `suggestion:<UUID>` and `reply_draft:<UUID>` for a step, call follow-up, update, suggestion card and reply draft. A suggestion uses the proposal ID that heads the card. A completion review uses the step's ID and mark version. An unavailable card returns `404 home_item_not_found`. A sent draft has no mark actions.

| `action`        | Extra fields and effect                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `snooze`        | `until`, a future instant with zone. The app picks the time for the chosen day in Central time.                                                           |
| `unsnooze`      | Clears the snooze disposition and time while keeping any waiting details.                                                                                 |
| `drop`          | Required nonblank `reason`. Permanently drops a call follow-up (`capture_post_call_action:`) or Home update (`notification:`, including `tour_feedback`). |
| `done`          | Marks a `tour_feedback` Home update read (`read_at = now()`), removing it from Home.                                                                      |
| `undo_done`     | Clears `read_at` on a `tour_feedback` Home update so it returns to Home.                                                                                  |
| `set-waiting`   | `target: { kind: "party", id }` and a nonblank `reason`. The ID is that of a person or company in the new database.                                       |
| `clear-waiting` | Clears the waiting person or company and reason.                                                                                                          |

The version is `<source change ID>:<mark change ID>:<visible disposition>`, where the visible disposition is `inbox`, `snoozed`, or `done` (for a read `tour_feedback` update). For a suggestion card, the source change ID comes from the latest change to its visible group, including decisions that remove members. A draft uses its own change history. Missing history starts at `0`. A cleared mark keeps its latest history ID. A change after the card was read returns `409 version_conflict { current_version }`. A valid instant that has already passed returns `422 invalid_snooze_time` on a snooze. Waiting changes leave the disposition intact. When both parts of a mark are empty, the save deletes its row. A dropped card records `drop_reason` with the partner's stamp in change history, clears any snooze or waiting mark, leaves Home for that user for good (no Undo), answers `200 { ok: true, item_id }`, and returns `404 home_item_not_found` to later mark saves.

For all other actions, the answer is `200 { ok, item_id, base_version, previous }`. The previous mark has `disposition`, `snoozed_until`, `waiting_on_party_id` and `waiting_reason` with nulls for absent values. Undo uses a fresh key, the returned version and the previous value for the affected part of the mark (`undo_done` for `tour_feedback` Done). Clear a prior snooze whose time has passed. Shared retry rules apply. App routing (slice 30) submits one request per card for a selection and refreshes Home after saves.

A suggestion group uses the most recent mark revision among its proposals, including a clear. A new suggestion in the group keeps its snooze until the saved time, even when the new proposal becomes the card's ID.
